# The Structural Sequence & Physics Test
**Type:** Load Bearing & Builder Logistics
**Target Agent:** `@foreman`

## Objective
Verify that the `WORKPLAN_[System].md` chronology perfectly respects the laws of physics, material curing times, and practical manual labor limits (the weekend warrior constraint).

## Verification Checklist

### 1. Concrete & Sub-Base Curing Physics
- [ ] Is there an explicit instruction to allow the min 100mm concrete slab to cure before taking heavy point loads (e.g., SIP panels or machinery)?
- [ ] Is the sub-base compacted (e.g., vibrating plate) before the XPS formwork is laid to prevent sinking?

### 2. The Wall & Roof Erection Sequence
- [ ] Are the wall panels completely joined, plumbed perfectly, and structurally glued before the continuous top plate is secured?
- [ ] Is the heavy roof lift (e.g., 175mm MgO SIPs spanning 4m) flagged as a "Big Event" requiring multiple strong mates or a genie lift?
- [ ] Is there explicit verification required from the manufacturer to ensure a 4m roof span does not need an intermediate structural Glulam beam?

### 3. Vibration & Heavy Machinery Physics
- [ ] Is the floating floor decoupled from the structural concrete slab so heavy Cast Iron machinery (e.g., Table Saws, Planers) can sit directly on the massive thermal battery, absorbing low-frequency vibration?
- [ ] Are specific vibration decoupling pads (e.g., Sylomer) specified exactly under the contact feet of high-frequency induction motors?

## Failure States (Do Not Pass If:)
- The Workplan instructs the builder to erect the walls immediately on the Sunday following the Saturday concrete pour.
- A 4x5m roof is slated to be lifted by a single person in a single weekend.
- The concrete perimeter XPS tub is poured without mini-line pumps or external bracing (if not self-supporting), risking hydrostatic blowout.
