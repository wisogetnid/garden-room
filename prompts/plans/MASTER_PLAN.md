# Garden Room Master Plan: 4x5m Cowes Workshop

## 1. Overview
This is the master blueprint for the high-end, sustainable 4x5m garden workshop, designed to meet or exceed Passivhaus standards.

## 2. Foundation System & Ground-to-Wall Interface
* **Type:** Floating Insulated Raft Foundation (Concrete slab fully enveloped in 100mm EPS L-elements and floor panels).
* **Thermal Performance:** Fully thermal-bridge-free.

### 2.1 Why a Structural Thermal Break is Needed on an Insulated Raft
You asked: *Why would I need a structural thermal break when using a floating raft insulation around the concrete slab, meaning the slab is now in the building envelope?*

Even with the concrete slab acting as an internal thermal mass separated from the ground, a structural thermal break (e.g., Marmox Thermoblock, Compacfoam, or Foamglas) is still strictly required beneath the timber sole plate. The reasons are:

1. **Flanking Transmission (Thermal Bypass):** Heat from the room enters the concrete slab. If the structural timber base track (which is a mild thermal bridge at ~0.14 W/mK) sits directly on the slab's perimeter, the heat flanks *around* the wall's main insulation by traveling through the slab and up into the timber track. Substituting the timber track with a high-performance thermal break ($\lambda \approx 0.04$ W/mK) severs this flanking path.
2. **Capillary Break & Damp Proofing (Critical DPC):** Concrete holds massive amounts of construction moisture for months and can absorb internal spills. Standard DPC plastic membranes are thin and easily punctured by structural fixings. A structural thermal break block is completely impermeable with zero capillarity, acting as an indestructible, physical Damp Proof Course (DPC) that keeps the vulnerable timber frame totally dry.
3. **Geometric Alignment (The L-Element Junction):** To maintain Passivhaus continuity, the external wall insulation must perfectly align with the foundation's EPS perimeter upstand. The structural wall must sit right at the edge of the slab. Since standard EPS cannot take the point load of a building, high-compressive structural thermal breaks span the gap between the hard concrete and soft EPS, rigidly supporting the wall precisely where it needs to sit without crushing the foundation insulation.

## 3. Material List & Estimated Quantities
*(Estimated for a 4x5m footprint / 20m² Workshop)*

### Foundation & Groundworks
* **Type 1 MOT Crushed Stone:** ~4-5 tonnes (for 150mm compacted sub-base)
* **Sand Blinding:** ~1 tonne (leveling layer)
* **EPS Insulated Raft System:** 20m² of 100mm high-density EPS floor panels + 18 linear meters of EPS L-shaped perimeter forms
* **DPM (1200 Gauge):** 1 roll (4m x 25m)
* **Concrete (C25/30):** ~2 cubic meters (100mm thick slab)
* **Steel Mesh (A193):** 3 sheets (4.8m x 2.4m)

### Structural & Thermal Break
* **Structural Thermal Break (Marmox Thermoblock / Compacfoam 200):** 18 linear meters (100mm wide)
* **High-Tack Polymer Sealant (e.g., CT1):** 4 tubes (for continuous capillary break bed)
* **C24 Structural Timber (100x45mm):** ~60 lengths (4.8m) for studs, top/sole plates (400mm centers)
* **OSB3 (12mm):** 16 sheets (for external wall sheathing)
* **C24 Timber Joists (150x45mm):** ~15 lengths for the roof (spaced 400mm)

### Insulation & Weatherproofing
* **PIR Insulation (100mm):** 16 sheets (for walls)
* **Rigid PIR Insulation (150mm):** 8 sheets (for warm roof deck)
* **Vapour Control Layer (Intello Plus):** 1 roll + Tescon Vana Tape
* **Breather Membrane:** 1 roll (1.5m x 50m)
* **EPDM Rubber Membrane:** 1 single-piece sheet (min 5.5m x 6.5m)
* **Treated Timber Battens (25x50mm):** ~40 lengths (rain-screen ventilation gap)
* **External Cladding (19mm Larch):** ~45m² coverage

## 4. Step-by-Step Construction Phases

### Phase 1: Groundworks & Floating Raft
1. Excavate topsoil and compact 150mm of Type 1 MOT.
2. Apply a sand blinding layer and lay the 1200g DPM.
3. Assemble the EPS L-elements around the perimeter and interlock the central 100mm EPS floor panels.
4. Position the A193 steel mesh on 50mm plastic chairs.
5. Pour 100mm of C25/30 concrete inside the EPS tub. Power-float or level the surface. Allow to cure.

### Phase 2: Frame & Ground-to-Wall Interface
6. **Thermal Break Installation:** Bed the structural thermal break (e.g., Compacfoam) continuously along the perimeter of the concrete slab using CT1 polymer sealant (acting as a perfect capillary break).
7. Erect the 100x45mm C24 timber wall panels directly onto the structural thermal break.
8. Install the 150x45mm roof joists with a 1:40 fall for drainage. Deck with 18mm OSB3.

### Phase 3: Weatherproofing & Envelope
9. Lay the VCL over the roof deck, install 150mm rigid PIR insulation (Warm Roof), and apply the single-piece EPDM membrane.
10. Sheathe the walls with 12mm OSB3 and wrap the exterior with the breather membrane, taping all joints.
11. Fix 25mm treated battens vertically to create the rain-screen cavity, ensuring top and bottom ventilation. Install external larch cladding.

### Phase 4: Insulation & Internal Fit-out
12. Friction-fit 100mm PIR boards between the internal wall studs.
13. Staple the continuous Vapour Control Layer (VCL) to the studs. **Crucial:** Tape the VCL down to the concrete slab / thermal break block using Tescon Vana tape to finalize airtightness.
14. Install 25mm internal battens over the VCL for the electrical service void. Finish with 15mm plasterboard or ply.

## 5. Potential Risks & Mitigation

1. **Damp-Proofing Errors (High Risk):** 
   - *Risk:* Puncturing the DPM or failing to provide a robust capillary break under the timber frame, allowing the concrete's construction moisture to rot the sole plate.
   - *Mitigation:* The Marmox/Compacfoam block acts as a physical, non-capillary barrier. Ensure the internal VCL overlaps the block and is taped down securely.
2. **Thermal Bypass / Flanking Transmission:**
   - *Risk:* Aligning the timber frame poorly with the foundation edge, leading to a cold bridge.
   - *Mitigation:* The structural thermal break must accurately bridge the gap between the concrete slab and the soft EPS perimeter upstand.
3. **Rain-Screen Ventilation Blockage:**
   - *Risk:* Blocking the 25mm cavity behind the cladding, causing trapped moisture and rotting battens.
   - *Mitigation:* Use stainless steel insect mesh at the bottom/top of the cavity to keep vermin out while allowing aggressive vertical airflow.
4. **Airtightness Failures:**
   - *Risk:* Interstitial condensation inside the walls due to gaps in the VCL (especially at window reveals or the floor junction).
   - *Mitigation:* Meticulous taping of all VCL joints. Conduct a blower-door test or smoke-pen test before plasterboarding.
