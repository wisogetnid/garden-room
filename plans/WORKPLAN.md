# SITE MANAGER'S WORKPLAN: Cowes High-Performance EPS-Core SIPS Workshop (3.6 x 5.116m)

**Status:** ACTIVE BUILD SCHEDULE  
**Foreman:** The DIY Project Coach  
**Target:** 14.53m² Net Usable Footprint (16.78m² Structural Core / 18.42m² Gross External) via 125mm Wall / 125mm Roof EPS-Core SIPs  
**Boundary Basis:** ≥1000mm from the North, South and East boundaries to the outermost projection (roof oversail + fascia + gutter). Core order size **3408 x 4924mm**.  

---

## 🏗️ The Foreman's Rules of Engagement

You're a weekend warrior with a 9-5 job and **45 to 90 minutes of daylight/energy on weeknights**. Burnout, not budget, is the biggest threat. Blow out your back on a Tuesday and the project stalls for a month.

1.  **The 'Big Event' Protocol:** Major milestones (Ground Screws, SIP Erection Handover, Roof Insulation & EPDM) get a 48-hour countdown checklist, weekends only, never alone. Get mates. Buy pizza.
2.  **Evening Micro-Shifts:** Weeknights are 45-minute setup or finesse tasks. Cowes has neighbours: quiet, low-impact work only (VCL taping, measuring, cutting insulation, tool maintenance). No chop saws after 7 PM.
3.  **The "Stop-Loss" Rule:** Past 90 minutes, or once tired mistakes start, tools down. Fatigue ruins materials and causes injuries.
4.  **The Sunday Lockdown:** 4:00 PM every Sunday, tools down. Run the 'Weather-Tight & Secure' checklist so the build stays dry and safe Monday to Friday. *With OSB SIPs, weather protection goes back on with zero delay.*
5.  **Statutory Position:** **Exempt from the Building Regulations** (Schedule 2, Class VI — confirmed in writing by Building Control) with a **Lawful Development Certificate**. No Building Control inspections. Keep every wall face ≥1000mm from the boundaries and build to the LDC drawings.

## Step-by-Step Construction Phases (Overview)
1. **Phase 1: Ground Screws & Foundation Platform** (Weeks 1-3): Ground screws (heads at -135mm as installed) with 10mm packers, 175mm heave void over the -300mm pit, 141x125mm ring beam (-125mm to 0mm = Datum Zero), 47x125mm C24 joists infilled with PIR, 22mm Caberdek P5 T&G sub-deck.
2. **Phase 2: Superstructure & Thermal Jacket** (Weeks 4-6 + weather hold): **New Forest SIPs supply and erect the shell** (DPC, sole plate, anchoring, 125mm SIP walls/roof) and wrap it in a temporary breather membrane. Weather hold of variable length. Then strip in stages: 80mm flat over-roof PIR + EPDM, upper XPS skirt (0 to +175mm; the starter track at -125mm and the lower strip went on in Weekend 2), 60mm Wood Fibre (+175mm upwards), Tyvek wrap.
3. **Phase 3: Glazing & Cladding** (Weeks 7-8): Quick-Connect AC wall penetration, D-AC acoustic double-glazed uPVC windows (West @ 2080mm FFL, clerestories @ 1997mm FFL), ~1000mm × ≤ ~2060mm single-leaf (900mm leaf) door on the continuous SIP sole plate (+40mm FFL), 25mm rainscreen battens, Hardie VL cladding, ventilated slatted gap cover, rainwater system.
4. **Phase 4: Internal Thermal Floor & Passivhaus Detailing** (Weeks 9-11): Peel the Caberdek film, glue 18mm birch plywood (BB/BB, exterior) to the 22mm Caberdek with D4 adhesive (40mm laminated slab), utility cable pull & airtight sealing, Tescon Vana VCL taping, 15mm direct-mount Fermacell, clear hard-wax oil / lacquer on the birch (FFL = +40mm).
5. **Phase 5: MEP, Climate & Final Commissioning** (Week 12+): Electrics, dMVHR (Zehnder ComfoAir 70 on a 60mm internal build-out, 270mm core, centre at +1471mm Datum Zero / 1431mm FFL), ASHP, TT Island, machine pads, commissioning.

## 🗓️ Phase 1: Ground Screws & Timber Frame (Weeks 1-3)
*No heavy digging. Let the pros drive the screws.*

### Week 1: Site Prep & Ground Screws
*   **Ground Screws:** Three concrete slabs lie buried 300mm down — leave them. A specialist contractor drives **14 No. 2050mm ground screws** on a **3268mm E-W × 4784mm N-S** grid (diagonal **~5793.5mm**), breaking out slab locally where a screw lands on one and working around the oak roots. ✅ **Engineer-approved layout (max 29kN factored per screw):** 10 under the ring beam, 4 under the internal E-W beams. Set out every screw from the MASTER_PLAN §1.2.1 coordinate table, measured from P01 (South-East corner), and check both diagonals before the rig starts. ✅ **As installed: screw heads at -135mm** (15mm above the -150mm design). The ring beam goes on **10mm packers** (galvanised or stainless shim plates, full top-plate seat) so its top lands at **Datum Zero (0mm)**; confirm the packing (or top-plate height adjustment) with the contractor (MASTER_PLAN §1.2). Agree the beam-to-plate fixing with the contractor beforehand (MASTER_PLAN §1.2).
*   **Weekend 1:** Clear surface vegetation and loose debris. Set up a level storage area for deliveries: concrete-block bearers on polythene, tarps and tie-downs ready (MASTER_PLAN §1.2.3).

### Week 2: Retaining, Weed Control & Timber Joists
*   **Pit Perimeter, Geotextile & Rodent Mesh: Sequence.** Boards and geotextile go in **before the ring beam** (Saturday and Sunday of the week before Weekend 2), while the pit is open and the screw heads are the only obstructions. The rodent mesh goes on **after the ring beam is bolted** (Weekend 2), because its top edge fixes to the ring beam outer face. Materials: Jewson order in `research/suppliers/jewson-pit-perimeter-order.md`. The 6mm mesh and the line pins come from elsewhere.
    *   **As dug (user, 13 Oct 2026):** the **South and East** sides already have a ~400mm trench at -300mm (measured from the screw centres, so ~330mm clear of the ring beam). On the **South** side, concrete stands ~300mm from the screws (~230mm clear). Both are inside the 150–400mm clearance band, so **neither needs boarding**. The **North** end is oversized and stays unboarded too. **Only the West face gets retaining boards.**
    *   **Geometry:** ring beam outer face = **70mm outside the screw centre lines** (3408 − 3268 = 140mm). West board **inner face** = 200mm outside the ring beam = **270mm outside the P03–P07–P11–P14 line**. The boards bear **directly on the soil** (no gap): the **West earth face sits 22mm behind the string = 292mm outside the screw line**. The boards are held by **16mm steel road form pins** on the pit side (no stakes, no pockets).

*   **Saturday: Set Out, Trim & Board the West Face (Stage 1 of 2)**
    1.  **Water check:** ✅ the pit stays dry, even after heavy rain (13 Oct 2026). Bail out any water from a fresh downpour and let the floor firm up before boarding.
    2.  **String the board line.** Run a string tight over the West screw centres (P03–P07–P11–P14). At both ends, measure **270mm square outward** with the 8m tape and drive a steel line pin into the ground past the North and South ends of the pit. Re-string on the pins. That string is the board inner face.
    3.  **Trim the West earth face** to **exactly 22mm behind the string** (one board thickness), plumb, **for the bottom 150mm only** (-300 to -150mm), along the whole West side and into the corners until it meets the oversized North end and the South trench. This is the **westward extension (~376mm)** if it isn't dug yet. Cut it with the spade against a straight board held on the string line, and check it with the 1200mm level held against the face: aim for **±5mm**, because every hollow is a gap behind the board. Cut slightly shy and pare to the line rather than over-dig; an over-cut has to be packed back (step 9). Barrow the spoil well away from the edge. **Above -150mm, cut the soil back at 45°** from the board line up to the ground (~150mm back at the surface). The slope gives working room in front of the ring beam until the top board goes on (Stage 2, Weekend 2). Pin a polythene or geotextile offcut over the slope if heavy rain is forecast.
    4.  **Check the floor** with the laser: **-300mm or deeper** everywhere, including the strip along the new West face. Rake out loose spoil, roots and sharp stones. Don't backfill low spots with clay.
    5.  **Measure the West run** at the board line. The pit is oversized at the North end, so the earth face may run past 5324mm. Board the full length of earth face that needs holding, and return a short board round a corner only if the clay there is crumbling.
    6.  **Drive the steel pins** (**16mm × 600mm pointed road form pins**, mild steel). They stand **in front of the boards, on the pit side**, touching the board line (string), plumb. Space them at **≤900mm centres**, with one 50mm in from each end and **one each side of every board joint** (~50mm from the joint: a 16mm bar can't carry two butted ends). That makes **~9 pins** on the ~5.4m run. Drive them with the club hammer **300mm into the pit floor**, so they stand ~300mm tall with tops at or just below the ground, **never above 0mm**. Each pin takes 16mm out of the 200mm gap.
        *   **Over a buried slab** (no driving possible): let the boards span the slab between pins on soil either side (**≤1.2m** gap; expect a few mm of bow). If the slab is wider, hire an SDS drill and drill a 16mm hole ~100mm into the slab for the pin, set in CT1.
    7.  **Board the face, bottom course only:** first treat the boards (UC3): **2 coats of preservative** on the soil face, both long edges and every cut, dry before fitting. Hang one continuous length of **450mm DPC** (~5.5m) down the trimmed face: fold **~22mm under** where the bottom edge will sit (an L only; never turn it up the pit face, that makes a trough) and lay the upper ~150mm loose up the 45° slope, held with 2–3 ground staples. Then one course of **150 × 22mm** treated gravel board (-300 to -150mm) sitting on the DPC fold on the pit floor, slid in **behind the pins**, flat against the DPC. The pins are already full height, ready for the top course. **Cut plan (~5.4m run):** bottom course = one 3.6m board + one ~1.8m piece. Top course (Stage 2) = the same, **with the short piece at the opposite end** so the joints stagger ≥1.8m. One 3.0m board cut in half gives both short pieces. Every joint lands between its two pins. The top course finishes flush with or just below the ground, **never above 0mm**; if the ground is lower than Datum Zero, rip it down to suit.
    8.  **No screws needed:** the soil presses the boards onto the pins. Check line against the string and plumb with the 1200mm level, and pack soil behind any board that sits off the pins. End-grain preservative on every cut.
    9.  **Close any gaps:** tap along the boards. A hollow sound means a void behind the board. Unscrew that board, pack the hollow with damp spoil rammed in with a batten end behind the DPC, and refix. If you over-cut badly in a spot, ram the soil back in thin layers before boarding rather than leaving a void. No pea gravel is needed behind the West boards. The Stage 2 top course gets the same check.
    *   *Foreman Note: the unboarded South, East and North faces stay as dug. Don't undercut them; trim any overhang so the faces stand at or slightly back from vertical, and keep traffic off the edges.*

*   **Sunday: Geotextile (Zero-Build-Up, Weed & Moisture Control)**
    1.  **Measure the pit floor first**, earth face to earth face: ~3938mm East-West (270 West + 3268 + 400 East) and ~5.5m North-South (South concrete to the oversized North face). **Cut two pieces** off the **Groundtex woven 11.1 × 4.5m roll** (Jewson LSGGWF45), each **floor width + ~860mm long**: ~460mm on the West (150 up the bottom board + ~210 up the 45° slope + 100 onto the ground) and 400mm on the East (300 up the earth face + 100 onto the ground). That is **~4.8m each** with the measurements above, leaving ~1.5m spare for patches. Lay them **across** the pit (East-West), Piece A at the South end. Trim Piece B in width so it laps A by **300mm** at mid-length; keep the offcut for patches.
        *   **Woven fabric frays at cut edges:** fold every free cut edge under by ~50mm before pegging. The X-cuts at the screws are covered by the Flashband collars.
        *   **It's stiff and won't drape:** fold the corners flat and add 1–2 extra ground staples (hammered in) at every fold and along the West board-to-slope crease.
        *   **It splits at a nick:** rake the floor clear of sharp stones first, and don't drag it over the screw plates.
    2.  **Lay Piece A** loose over the floor, centred. At each of the screws in its zone, cut an **"X" just big enough to pass the 220mm top plate**, push the membrane down over it to the floor and wrap the flaps round the shaft. Collar each with **two turns of 100mm Flashband** (half on the shaft, half on the membrane). Patch any oversized cut with an offcut laid underneath, lapped 150mm.
    3.  **Tension and peg:** pull it flat, then peg along the foot of every face at **600mm** with galvanised 100 × 150mm ground staples (Toolstation 79016). Peg the open field at ~1m on bare soil.
    4.  **Over the slabs:** no staples. The membrane lying across a slab needs no fixing (nothing moves it in a closed void). **Don't rely on plain glue:** polypropylene is one of the few plastics hybrid adhesives like CT1 don't bond to.
        *   **Plan the lap off the slabs:** set Piece B's width so the 300mm lap lands on soil, where it can be stapled.
        *   **Where an edge must end on concrete:** use **"rivet" dabs**. Put a CT1 dab under the edge, press the fabric in so the adhesive squeezes up through the weave, then cover it with a second dab on top. The cured plug locks the fabric mechanically. Space them at ~300mm.
        *   Alternatively, masonry screws + penny washers (MASTER_PLAN §1.1) if an SDS or hammer drill is to hand.
    5.  **Up the faces:**
        *   **West (bottom board + slope):** lift the loose DPC flap off the slope first. Fold the membrane up the board face (between the board and the pins), over its top and up the 45° slope onto the ground, then **lay the DPC flap back on top of the membrane** (so the DPC can later stand up behind the top board, with the membrane behind it). Fix it just below the board top with **SPAX A2 4 × 50mm + A2 M6 penny washer** into the board at ~900mm. The Stage 2 top board later goes in front of it, so nothing needs undoing.
        *   **East and North (earth):** lay it up the earth face and ~100mm onto the ground, pegged at the top lip at **500mm**.
        *   **South (concrete):** run it to the foot of the concrete and ~100mm up its face, held with CT1 rivet dabs at ~300mm (step 4). If the floor at the foot is soil, staple there instead and stop the fabric at the foot.
        *   Fold the corners as "hospital corners" (folded, not cut). On the West, the ground lip ends **~250mm beyond the ring beam line** (150mm past the external cladding line). On the East, the 330mm trench already covers that. The future gravel margin covers the lips.
    6.  **Lay Piece B** the same way, lapping A by 300mm (B on top). Staple the lap at **300mm** (it should land on soil; see step 4).
    7.  **Check:** no exposed soil anywhere in the pit, no loose flaps, every screw collar sealed, no gravel ballast (saves 28mm of heave void). Floor still -300mm or below.
    8.  **Lockdown:** nothing to protect from rain at this stage; the membrane is permeable.
*   **Weekend 2 — Ring Beam & Joists:**
    *   **Cut List (MASTER_PLAN §1.2.2):** 6 × 5.4m cut to 4924mm (East/West), 6 × 3.6m cut to 3408mm (South/North), 6 × 3.6m cut to 3125mm (internal beams, square ends, dry-fit tight between the ring beams). Every ply runs full length, no butt joints.
    *   Build the 141x125mm ring beam and the two internal E-W beams: sash-clamp 3 lengths of 47x125mm C24 and laminate with **SPAX 8 × 140mm A2 countersunk T40 screws** (or the engineer-approved **Swiftfix 8 × 140mm A4** if SPAX can't deliver in time — trial two in an offcut, pre-drill if the outer ply splits). Alternating faces at 150mm centres (300mm per face): outer face 45mm from the top, inner face 45mm from the bottom, ≥100mm from cut ends, 2 extra screws each side of butt joints, butt joints only over screw positions (MASTER_PLAN §1.2 Lamination Screws).
    *   **Halved Corners:** On the laminated beams, cut a **141mm × 62.5mm** halving at each end — top half off the East/West beams, bottom half off the South/North beams (saw kerfs to depth, chisel clean). Drive the East/West inner-face lamination screws inside the halving now; access closes once the corner is assembled. Two coats of end-grain preservative on every cut face and cross-cut; let it dry. Dry-fit each corner and check it finishes flush at 0mm (±3mm) before fixing.
    *   Fit the 10mm packers on every top plate and level them to -125mm with a laser. Ring beam sits -125mm to 0mm; Fix the East/West beams down to the 220mm top plates (**[OPEN: fixing method to confirm with No More Digging, see MASTER_PLAN §1.2]**). Drop the South/North beams onto the halvings and fix each corner with **4 × 8 × 100mm stainless structural screws** driven down, heads countersunk flush. At P04/P07/P08/P11 the plate fixing goes on the **outer face only** (one bracket; the internal beam covers the inner face), plus coach screws from below if the plate holes allow. Drop each internal beam in tight between the East/West beams (no notches), centred on its screw line, and drive **2 × 8 × 160mm fully threaded screws per end** at 45° from the beam top, 55mm back from the end, towards the ring beam (drilling guide, heads flush). No inner-face lamination screws within 100mm of these joints. Check both diagonals again.
    *   **Joist set-out:** mark the joist centres on the South/North ring beams and both faces of the internal beams, measured from the East ring beam inner face: **23.5 (edge), 365.6, 707.7, 1049.8, 1391.9, 1734.0, 2076.2, 2418.3, 2760.4, 3102.5 (edge)** (~342mm centres, 10 lines per bay, MASTER_PLAN §1.2). Hang the 8 inner lines of 47x125mm joists (cut ~1448mm, one per bay) on **Simpson SAE250/47/2** face-fix hangers (101.5mm tall — never a taller hanger, it would stand proud of the deck), flush with the top (0mm). Screw the two edge joist lines tight to the East/West ring beam with **8 × 140mm stainless screws at 300mm, staggered 35mm from top and bottom**, midway between the ring beam's inner-face lamination screws. Single joists throughout: no twins at the internal screw lines. If a heavy machine's position is known, fit its sister joists now (MASTER_PLAN §3.1). **No restraint straps or noggins:** the edge joists and the deck tie the long ring beams in (MASTER_PLAN §3.1 Floor Diaphragm Tie). Fix galvanised angle brackets (~40 × 40 × 3mm, 2 per bay) to the South/North ring beam inner faces between the hangers, horizontal leg flush at 0mm, to carry the deck edge. Joist undersides at -125mm give the 175mm heave void above the -300mm pit floor.
    *   *Foreman Note: Face-Fix hangers keep the ring beam top 100% timber, so no metal tabs puncture the DPC or pinch the sole plate.*
*   **Starter Track & Lower XPS Strip (Weekend 2, ring beam bolted, before the mesh):** the plinth insulation now wraps the ring beam face (MASTER_PLAN §2.3B).
    1.  **Starter track:** 60mm aluminium EWI starter track with drip edge, screwed to the ring beam outer face with its base **flush with the beam underside (-125mm)**, levelled with the laser. It clears the 220mm top plates (-135mm) by ~10mm; check the drip nose clears every plate edge.
    2.  **Cut the XPS:** rip the 60mm XPS boards (1250 × 600mm) into **two 300mm strips**. Cut each strip into a **125mm lower strip** (now) and a **175mm upper strip** (Week 6).
    3.  **Fit the lower strip** (-125 to 0mm) into the track, bonded to the beam face with low-expansion PU foam adhesive. Top **flush with the beam top (0mm), never proud** (the DPC and sole plate land there). Butt joints tight and foam-filled; notch round any beam-to-plate fixing on the face. The lower strip runs through the door opening; only the upper strip stops at the jambs.
*   **Rodent Mesh (The 'L-Shape' Defense):** fit it straight after the lower XPS strip, **in front of the XPS** (rats and mice burrow into XPS). Wear cut-resistant gloves: cut mesh edges are razor sharp.
    1.  **Cut strips:** split each 900mm-wide roll lengthwise into **two 450mm strips** with aviation snips, tight against a wire so no long spikes are left. Two 6m rolls give 24m of strip for the ~17m perimeter + laps.
    2.  **Bend the "L":** clamp the strip between a ring beam offcut and the bench edge and fold along a wire line: **~270mm vertical + ~150mm foot** (~130mm on the West, step 5).
    3.  **Hang:** start at a corner. Top edge at **~-30mm** on the XPS face, just below the slat tops (-25mm), so it stays hidden; the gap-cover wall ledger later clamps it tight from -51mm. It needs to go no higher: the void starts below the beam (-125mm), and the mesh above that only shields the XPS in the dark strip under the cover. Above ground, the XPS's UV finish protects it. Fix through the mesh and XPS into the beam with **SPAX A2 6 × 100mm washer-head T30 screws (Jewson SXSS6010) + A2 M6 penny washers at 300mm**, in a row at **-110mm**, below the gap-cover wall ledger band (-51 to -101mm). Snug the washers onto the XPS without crushing it.
    4.  **Past the track and plates:** the XPS face is 60mm out and the plates ~40mm, so the mesh drops **straight down** over the track's drip nose and past every plate, with no step-out. Check each perimeter plate: if one sticks out more than ~55mm past the beam face (screw off position, offset plate), dress the mesh over it as a local bulge. **Never cut a slot round a plate.**
    5.  **Foot:** flat on the geotextile, pointing **outward**, stapled at **300mm** through mesh and membrane together **where the floor is soil**. Over a slab, staples won't go in: hold the foot flat with **CT1 dabs at ~600mm** (rats can't dig through the slab) and staple tight either side of every slab-to-soil edge, where they would dig. On the West, the gap from the XPS face to the boards is 140mm, so trim the foot to **~130mm**.
    6.  **Laps and corners:** overlap strips by **100mm**, screwed through both layers on the XPS and stapled through both on the foot. At corners, mitre the foot, fold the vertical leg round with a 100mm return and screw it.
    7.  **Test:** walk the perimeter with a 6mm drill bit. If it passes through anywhere (lap, corner, over the track, the gap between foot and floor), patch it.
*   **West Face, Stage 2 (Weekend 2, straight after the mesh):** the 45° slope gave ~270–320mm in front of the ring beam at the screw heights (mesh row -110mm, ledger -51 to -101mm), against 200mm (140mm in front of the XPS) with both boards on.
    1.  **Use the room first:** fix the West track, lower XPS strip and mesh (above), then the **gap-cover wall ledger** now: 47 × 50mm, -51 to -101mm, on the XPS face over the mesh. Fix it with **SPAX A2 6 × 140mm washer-head T30 screws** (Jewson SXSS6014) at **300mm**, through ledger, mesh and XPS into the beam (~32mm bite) (Week 8 step 2). A 140mm screw can't be driven sideways in the 140mm gap later.
    2.  **Pre-assemble the top board on the bench:** screw the gap-cover **outer ledger** (47 × 50mm treated, top at -51mm when fitted, so 51mm below the board's top edge if that sits at 0mm) onto the board's pit face, **driving the screws from the back of the board** (SPAX A2 4 × 50mm at 300mm). The board + ledger then goes in as one unit.
    3.  **Drop the top board in** from above, **behind the pins** and in front of the DPC and membrane on the slope, sitting on the bottom board. Lift the **DPC** up flat against the back of the top board (it stays between the board and the backfill). The pins hold it; no screws. Cut a **20mm groove** across the back of the outer ledger at each pin position before pre-assembly (step 2), so the ledger passes over the pins.
    4.  **Backfill the wedge** behind the DPC (between the DPC and the membrane on the slope) with damp spoil, rammed in 50mm layers with a batten end, up to the ground. Fold the membrane's ground lip back over the top. Tap-test the top course for voids.
    5.  **Cap the top edge:** fold the DPC over the board's top edge and **~30mm down its pit face** (stop above the outer ledger at -51mm; slit it round any pin top). Fix with **SPAX A2 4 × 45mm + A2 M6 penny washers at ~600mm**. Trim the surplus. The cap sheds drips from the slats; the bottom stays an open L so nothing can pond.
*   **Insulation Support & Drop:** Make a **height jig**: a 125mm joist offcut with a stop screwed flush with its bottom edge. Working from above, in each pocket hold the jig against the joist side, seat an **A2 stainless angle bracket** (25mm wide, ~35mm legs) on the stop with its horizontal leg pointing into the pocket and flush with the joist underside (-125mm), and fix it with one A2 4 × 40mm T20 screw ~90mm below the joist top (compact driver or right-angle adapter). **3 per side per pocket (~500mm apart) + one each side of every piece end.** No timber battens (they would hang into the heave void). Measure each **125mm PIR** board's thickness; rasp any proud board flush on top and re-tape the foil. Rip each board into **four ~293mm strips**, cut to the pocket lengths (one 1450mm piece + one 950mm offcut per strip; pair the 950s with 500mm offcuts in the remaining pockets). Drop the strips onto the brackets, flush with the joist tops, and seal all four edges and every butt joint with **low-expansion** PU foam (Soudal Flexible, Toolstation; or Soudafoam Gun Grade, Screwfix). Notch the board top 3mm under each deck-edge bracket. Only open as many bays as you can deck or tarp that weekend.

### Week 3: The Weatherproof Sub-Deck
*   **Weekend 3:** Snap a line **135mm in from the ring beam outer face** all round: the deck stops there, leaving the 125mm sole plate zone + 10mm gap clear for NFS. Lay 22mm Caberdek P5 T&G chipboard (peel-off film, ~21kg solo lift) at 0mm, D4 glue in every T&G joint, SPAX WIROX 4.5 × 60mm screws at 300mm centres into joists, **150mm along the two East/West edge joists** (diaphragm tie). Leave the film on. This is the dry working platform for the superstructure (rated 42 days exposed).
*   **Sunday Lockdown (Weeks 2–3):** Move the 4 × 10m tarp from the (now used) timber stack to the platform: 4m across the width, surplus folded back at the ends, ridge over an offcut, tied down through every eyelet to blocks or sandbags. Timber and board stacks stay on bearers under their own tarps (MASTER_PLAN §1.2.3).

## 🗓️ Phase 2: Superstructure & Thermal Jacket (Weeks 4-6)
*New Forest SIPs build the shell. Hand them a perfect platform, check their work, and never leave bare OSB exposed once their wrap comes off.*

### Week 4 Prep: Platform Handover to New Forest SIPs
*   **Weeknights (Quiet, 45m-1.5h):** Check the platform before NFS arrive — the last time a mistake is cheap. Ring beam outer footprint **3408 × 4924mm**, diagonals equal, top level at **0mm (Datum Zero)** all round (±3mm). Deck screwed down and swept. Clear the delivery and handling route (Yarmouth ferry, shuttle vehicle for the final mile). Send NFS the targets: **SIP wall and sole plate outer faces flush with the ring beam outer face** (~16mm ledge inside), East/West top plates **+2216.7 / +2273.5mm** (wall panels **2216.7 / 2273.5mm** from the 0mm sole plate), 120mm roof oversail all round, and the factory-cut **270mm dMVHR core** position with spline clearance (MASTER_PLAN §4.2.1).

### Week 4: 🚨 BIG EVENT: NFS Shell Erection & Handover
*   **NFS on site:** DPC, sole plates, anchoring, wall and roof panels with all structural connections, wrapped in their **temporary breather membrane**.
*   **Handover Checklist (before they leave):**
    *   Walls plumb; outer faces flush with the ring beam; wall plan diagonals equal.
    *   Top plates: East **+2216.7mm**, West **+2273.5mm**; roof falls **1:60** to the East; **120mm** oversail on all four sides.
    *   Every opening (door, windows, clerestories, 270mm dMVHR core) in position and size — check against the MASTER_PLAN §1.0a opening schedule (panel-base heights).
    *   Wrap: laps shingled (upper over lower), taped closed over **every opening**, no loose flaps, roof shedding water at 1:60.
    *   Get from NFS: the membrane **product name and rated UV-exposure period**, and their fixing schedule.

### Weather Hold (Variable Length — Work Commitments)
*   The NFS wrap buys time, not unlimited time. 🚨 *Foreman Note: a breather membrane is not a roof; on a 1:60 roof, water sits on it. Roof PIR + EPDM is the first job after the hold. Over-sheet the roof with a tarp if the hold runs into wet or windy weeks.*
*   **Weekly walk-round (15 min):** lifted laps, wind damage, ponding on the roof; re-tape tears with Tescon Extora. After any named storm, check the same day.
*   **UV deadline:** count days from handover against the membrane's rated UV exposure. If it will overrun, sheet walls and roof with heavy-duty tarpaulins before the deadline.

### Week 5: 🚨 BIG EVENT: Roof Insulation & Weather-Tight Roof (one dry weekend)
*   **Forecast:** dry 2-day window only — the roof OSB is exposed once the wrap comes off.
*   **Saturday (Strip & Kerb):** Strip the NFS membrane from the **roof only**, in sections. Confirm the **1:60** fall across the short width. Tape the external roof panel joints. Fix the 80mm timber kerb batten on edge around the perimeter. If rain threatens, tarp the bare sections before stopping.
*   **Sunday (Weather-Tight):** Bond flat 80mm high-compressive PIR (Kingspan TR27) to the roof SIP's top OSB skin. Glue the 1.5mm EPDM onto the PIR.
*   **Sunday Lockdown:** Fit UPVC drip edge and kerb trims. The roof is permanently weather-tight. The NFS wrap stays on the walls until Week 6.

### Week 6: External Insulation, Upper XPS Skirt & Tyvek Wrap
*   **Stripping Rule (One Face at a Time):** Strip the NFS membrane from **one elevation**, then insulate and Tyvek it before stopping. Never end a day with bare OSB on a wall. Start with the windward West face in good weather; leave sheltered faces for later weekends if needed.
*   **Ground Clearance (MASTER_PLAN §2.3B):** the base sits at 0mm (lawn level). Keep the gap cover and the finished gravel margin **below 0mm**, falling away from the wall, and **10mm clear of the XPS face**; nothing (lawn, soil, paving) bears against the XPS skirt.
*   **Upper XPS Skirt:** the starter track and lower strip (-125 to 0mm) went on in Weekend 2. Fix the **60 × 175mm upper XPS strips** (0 to **+175mm**, cut from the same boards) on top of the lower strip, foam-sealing the joint at 0mm: 100mm wood screws with plastic washers into the sole plate / SIP base, backed by PU adhesive. Stop at the door jambs.
*   **Wood Fibre Insulation:** Fix **60mm Rigid Wood Fibre** to the SIP OSB from **+175mm** (on top of the XPS) with the manufacturer's **temporary insulation fixings** (plastic disc + screw, 2–4 per board) until the Week 8 battens clamp it. Seal the XPS/Wood Fibre joint with Compriband tape or vapour-permeable MS Polymer sealant.
*   **dMVHR Opening in the Wood Fibre:** Push a long drill bit out through the centre of the factory-cut 270mm SIP core to mark the axis outside. Cut a **270mm circle** through the Wood Fibre (handsaw, insulation knife or multi-tool). Tyvek over it; it is opened in Week 7.
*   **Breather Membrane:** Drape **Tyvek** over the Wood Fibre and the upper XPS skirt, **ending at 0mm** on the XPS face just above the rodent mesh, bottom edge left free so water drains down the XPS to the starter track drip (-125mm). That elevation is now permanently protected. Repeat face by face until the NFS wrap is gone.

## 🗓️ Phase 3: Glazing, Cladding & Rainwater (Weeks 7-8)

### Week 7: AC Penetration, Glazing & Threshold Detailing
*   **AC Core Hole (Before Cladding):** Mount the indoor bracket for the Quick-Connect AC on the SIP OSB. Core ~65mm through the SIP and Wood Fibre at a 2-3° fall to the outside (condensate). Pass the pipe bundle out and leave it coiled. Foam the core gap airtight; seal inside with Tescon Vana / Roflex grommet.
*   **Service Cable Core Hole (Before Cladding):** Core ~32mm through the SIP and Wood Fibre behind the Consumer Unit, clear of the 1200mm spline joints, 1-2° fall to the outside. Seal the outside onto the Tyvek with a Pro Clima Kaflex grommet (or Tescon Extora tape). The electrician runs the SWA and Cat6a along the fence and dresses them in with a drip loop below the entry.
*   **dMVHR Wall Pipe (Before Cladding):** Cut the Zehnder Ø250mm wall pipe to the **296mm** finished wall at the unit (236mm wall + 60mm build-out; Zehnder template for exact projection). Cross-cut the Tyvek over the hole and fold back the flaps. Slide the pipe in at a **1° fall to the outside**, shim it level side-to-side in the 20mm annulus, and fill the SIP core and Wood Fibre gap with Illbruck FM330. Seal the Tyvek to the pipe with an **EPDM / Tescon Extora collar**. Cap both pipe ends. *Foreman Note: check the fall with a digital level before the foam cures — it can't be corrected afterwards.*
*   **Glazing & Door:** Install the six D-AC uPVC fixed windows and the ~1000mm × ≤ ~2060mm single-leaf (900mm leaf), half-glazed thermally broken aluminium door (double-glazed upper panel, insulated lower panel) in the factory-cut openings.
    *   *Door seals (MASTER_PLAN §2.7):* gaskets continuous and compressed on all four sides; threshold seal closes fully onto the flush +40mm threshold (no daylight with the door shut).
    *   *Door movement detail (MASTER_PLAN §2.7):* ~10mm jamb and ~15mm head gaps; jamb fixings only (packer behind each, nothing rigid through the head); expanding foam tape plus internal Contega with a movement fold at the head; never gun-foam alone. Record the opening diagonals and sill level in the site journal for re-checking after the first winter and summer.
    *   *Handling:* D-AC units weigh ~37 kg/m² of glass (a 1000x1000mm West unit is ~30 kg of glass alone). 2-person lift with heavy-duty suction cups, mandatory.
    *   *Expansion gap:* 10-12mm perimeter clearance around every frame (uPVC moves ~3× more than aluminium; a 1500mm clerestory moves ~4mm over a 40°C swing — follow the profile maker's guidance), sealed with pre-compressed expanding foam tape (Compriband / Illbruck illmod 600 / TP652) and low-expansion flexible PU foam, Pro Clima Tescon Vana inside.
    *   *Datums:* North/South/East clerestory tops at **1997mm FFL** (179.7mm head zone under the +2216.7mm plate); West windows (1000x1000mm) and door tops at **2080mm FFL** (153.5mm head zone under the +2273.5mm plate).
*   **Threshold & Drainage:** The SIP sole plate runs through the door opening and carries the frame. Check its top level across the opening: if it is above **+40mm FFL** (e.g., +47mm), plane / chisel it down to +40mm and brush-treat the cut surface with preservative before bedding the frame (MASTER_PLAN §2.7). Set an Aco HexDrain channel flush with the patio in front of the West door; apply **RIW Flexiseal** over the threshold junction.

### Week 8: Rainscreen Battens, Cladding & Rainwater System
*   **Rainscreen Battens:** **25x50mm treated battens @ 400mm centres**, vertical over the Tyvek, 150mm A4 EWI screws at alternating 60°/90° (Truss-Screw method). Staple **Perforated Bug Mesh** across the bottom 25mm gap (cavity inlet at 0mm). **Fit the ~18.6 lm top vent trim now:** Perforated Aluminium Vent Trim stapled to the roof SIP soffit across the 25mm batten gap (MASTER_PLAN §2.6 item 5). *Foreman Note: the cavity's only air outlet — it MUST go on before the top Hardie plank, which blocks access.*
*   **dMVHR Batten Frame:** Short 25mm batten noggins framing a **~280 × 280mm square** centred on the pipe, with gaps top and bottom so the cavity drains and ventilates.
*   **Cladding:** **James Hardie VL Fibre Cement Cladding** on the battens with Hardie™ Plank Screws (4.2x40mm T15 Torx), drill/driver on low torque (no impact drivers).
    *   *dMVHR notch:* The Zehnder outside panel (380 × 376mm) hides the opening, so cut a **~280 × 280mm square** across the planks that cross the pipe. Mark each plank in place, take it down, cut on trestles: long straight cuts with the fibre-cement circular saw blade (or score-and-snap), ~10mm relief hole at each inside corner, short cuts with a jigsaw (carbide-grit blade) or multi-tool. 🚨 *Silica: outdoors only, on-tool extraction, FFP3 mask.* Leave the outside wall panel off until the unit is hung (Week 12+).
*   **Ventilated Gap Cover (Slatted Lid, MASTER_PLAN §1.1):** closes the open gap between the XPS skirt and the pit edge once the cladding is on. Until then, lay scaffold boards across the gap wherever you walk or stand a ladder.
    1.  **Levels:** slat tops at **-25mm** (25mm under the starter track, below the lawn datum). Slat undersides and **all ledger tops at -51mm**. Mark -51mm all round with the laser.
    2.  **Wall ledger:** 47 × 50mm treated, against the **XPS face** (60mm out from the ring beam), **-51 to -101mm**, over the rodent mesh (it clamps the mesh a second time). Fix with **SPAX A2 6 × 140mm washer-head T30 screws (SXSS6014) at 300mm** through ledger, mesh and XPS into the beam, 2 per length within 100mm of each end. The XPS carries the slats and the odd footstep easily. (The West ledger is already on, from Weekend 2.)
    3.  **Pit-side ledger, same level (top -51mm):**
        *   **West:** already fitted. It was pre-screwed onto the top board in Stage 2, and the West wall ledger went on in Weekend 2. Just check both are at -51mm.
        *   **East and North (earth faces):** 47 × 50 × 600mm treated pointed stakes driven against the face at **≤900mm**, ledger screwed to them with 2 × SPAX A2 6 × 100mm washer-head T30 (SXSS6010) per stake.
        *   **South (concrete):** check the concrete top level. If it sits at or below -51mm and is sound, bed the ledger on CT1 against or on the concrete. If not, use stakes in front as on the East.
        *   The ledger ends meet at the corners.
    4.  **Slats:** **26 × 118mm treated redwood decking** (Jewson PR321242, 4.2m), **perpendicular to the wall**, grooved face up. **15mm gaps**, set with a 15mm spacer offcut. Cut each slat to the distance from the XPS face to the pit-side edge, less **10mm at the wall end** (a drip and air slot in front of the mesh) and **5mm at the pit end**: about 125mm West, 155mm South, 255mm East, North as measured. Brush end-grain preservative on every cut.
    5.  **Corners:** run the slats of the long sides (East/West) straight through the corner squares. Screw a short 47 × 50mm block between the two ledger ends to support them.
    6.  **Fix:** 2 × SPAX A2 4 × 50mm per slat end into each ledger, 15mm in from the slat edges. The cover lifts slat by slat with a driver: no glue.
    7.  **Check:** slats flat and level at -25mm (±3mm), no slat end unsupported, the 10mm slot in front of the XPS is open (water running down the wall drops through it to the track drip at -125mm), and you can see down through every gap. The gravel margin then starts at the pit edge, at or below -25mm and falling away from the building.
*   **Rainwater Harvesting:** 112mm UPVC Deepflow guttering, Sure-Edge drip trims, downpipe diverter, 200L slimline water butt.

## 🗓️ Phase 4: Internal Thermal Floor & Passivhaus Detailing (Weeks 9-11)
*All work inside the dry, weather-tight building.*

### Week 9: Solid Timber Diaphragm Deck
*   **Birch Plywood Load-Spreader & Floor:** Peel the Caberdek film. Spread D4 structural adhesive and lay **18mm exterior birch plywood BB/BB** (2440 × 1220mm, square edge, ~36kg a sheet — two-person lift) over the 22mm Caberdek, joints staggered ≥300mm from the Caberdek joints, screwed into joists at 300mm centres — a solid 40mm laminated slab. Countersink the screws neatly; this face is the finished floor. Cover it with hardboard or Correx sheet for the rest of the fit-out.
*   **Utility Penetration:** Pull the SWA and Cat6a through the service core hole. Fill the SIP core void with Illbruck FM330 and seal the inside face to the SIP OSB with a Pro Clima Kaflex grommet / Tescon Vana tape before the Fermacell.

### Week 10: Internal Passivhaus Airtight Taping
*   **Airtight VCL Taping:** Tape every internal wall-to-wall, wall-to-roof and wall-to-floor joint, and all window and door reveals, with **Pro Clima Tescon Vana**. Tape the dMVHR pipe to the SIP OSB with overlapping Tescon Vana petals all round. This is the continuous airtight/vapour layer.

### Week 11: Direct-Mount Internal Boards & Floor Finish
*   **Direct-Mount Fermacell:** Pre-cut 15mm Fermacell, Jointstik on the edges, screwed to the taped SIP OSB with 30mm Fermacell screws (REI30/REI60 boundary fire rating).
*   **dMVHR Internal Build-Out:** With the main Fermacell up (cut neatly round the pipe), build the **60mm build-out**: 45 × 45mm planed treated timber frame screwed through the Fermacell into the SIP OSB, 40mm wood fibre infill, 15mm Fermacell face glued and screwed, boxed tight to the pipe. ≥50mm beyond the unit's mounting plate on every side (~600 × 700mm — Zehnder template). Finished wall at the pipe **≥275mm** (target 296mm).
*   **Floor Finish:** Lift the protection, vacuum, and apply clear hard-wax oil or floor lacquer to the birch plywood (FFL = +40mm). Do this after the dusty work.

## 🛑 The Foreman’s Final Warning
You are building a high-tech thermal envelope, not a shed.
*   Wet OSB swells and loses structural integrity. Protect it at all costs.
*   The Fermacell is the boundary fire protection. Don't skip it.
*   Pace yourself. Eat well. Keep the site clean so you don't trip over a chop saw on a Sunday evening.
