# SITE MANAGER'S WORKPLAN: Cowes High-Performance EPS-Core SIPS Workshop (3.6 x 5.116m)

**Status:** ACTIVE BUILD SCHEDULE  
**Foreman:** The DIY Project Coach  
**Target:** 14.53m² Net Usable Footprint (16.78m² Structural Core / 18.42m² Gross External) via 125mm Wall / 125mm Roof EPS-Core SIPs  
**Boundary Basis:** ≥1000mm from the North, South and East boundaries to the outermost projection (roof oversail + fascia + gutter). Core order size **3408 x 4924mm**.  

---

## 🏗️ The Foreman's Rules of Engagement

You're a weekend warrior with a 9-5 job and **45 to 90 minutes of daylight/energy on weeknights**. Burnout, not budget, is the biggest threat. Blow out your back on a Tuesday and the project stalls for a month.

1.  **The 'Big Event' Protocol:** Major milestones (Ground Screws, SIP Erection Handover, Roof Insulation & EPDM) get a 48-hour countdown checklist, weekends only, never alone. Get mates. Buy pizza.
2.  **Evening Micro-Shifts:** Weeknights are 45-minute setup or finesse tasks. Cowes has neighbours: quiet, low-impact work only (VCL taping, measuring, cutting insulation, tool maintenance). No chop saws after 7 PM.
3.  **The "Stop-Loss" Rule:** Past 90 minutes, or once tired mistakes start, tools down. Fatigue ruins materials and causes injuries.
4.  **The Sunday Lockdown:** 4:00 PM every Sunday, tools down. Run the 'Weather-Tight & Secure' checklist so the build stays dry and safe Monday to Friday. *With OSB SIPs, weather protection goes back on with zero delay.*
5.  **Statutory Position:** **Exempt from the Building Regulations** (Schedule 2, Class VI — confirmed in writing by Building Control) with a **Lawful Development Certificate**. No Building Control inspections. Keep every wall face ≥1000mm from the boundaries and build to the LDC drawings.

## Step-by-Step Construction Phases (Overview)
1. **Phase 1: Ground Screws & Foundation Platform** (Weeks 1-3): Ground screws (heads at -135mm as installed) with 10mm packers, 175mm heave void over the -300mm pit, 141x125mm ring beam (-125mm to 0mm = Datum Zero), 47x125mm C24 joists infilled with PIR, 22mm Caberdek P5 T&G sub-deck.
2. **Phase 2: Superstructure & Thermal Jacket** (Weeks 4-6 + weather hold): **New Forest SIPs supply and erect the shell** (DPC, sole plate, anchoring, 125mm SIP walls/roof) and wrap it in a temporary breather membrane. Weather hold of variable length. Then strip in stages: 80mm flat over-roof PIR + EPDM, 0mm aluminium starter track, 180mm XPS skirt (0mm to +180mm), 60mm Wood Fibre (+180mm upwards), Tyvek wrap.
3. **Phase 3: Glazing & Cladding** (Weeks 7-8): Quick-Connect AC wall penetration, D-AC acoustic double-glazed uPVC windows (West @ 2080mm FFL, clerestories @ 1997mm FFL), ~1000mm × ≤ ~2060mm single-leaf (900mm leaf) door on the continuous SIP sole plate (+40mm FFL), 25mm rainscreen battens, Hardie VL cladding, rainwater system.
4. **Phase 4: Internal Thermal Floor & Passivhaus Detailing** (Weeks 9-11): Peel the Caberdek film, glue 18mm birch plywood (BB/BB, exterior) to the 22mm Caberdek with D4 adhesive (40mm laminated slab), utility cable pull & airtight sealing, Tescon Vana VCL taping, 15mm direct-mount Fermacell, clear hard-wax oil / lacquer on the birch (FFL = +40mm).
5. **Phase 5: MEP, Climate & Final Commissioning** (Week 12+): Electrics, dMVHR (Zehnder ComfoAir 70 on a 60mm internal build-out, 270mm core, centre at +1471mm Datum Zero / 1431mm FFL), ASHP, TT Island, machine pads, commissioning.

## 🗓️ Phase 1: Ground Screws & Timber Frame (Weeks 1-3)
*No heavy digging. Let the pros drive the screws.*

### Week 1: Site Prep & Ground Screws
*   **Ground Screws:** Three concrete slabs lie buried 300mm down — leave them. A specialist contractor drives **14 No. 2050mm ground screws** on a **3268mm E-W × 4784mm N-S** grid (diagonal **~5793.5mm**), breaking out slab locally where a screw lands on one and working around the oak roots. ✅ **Engineer-approved layout (max 29kN factored per screw):** 10 under the ring beam, 4 under the internal E-W beams. Set out every screw from the MASTER_PLAN §1.2.1 coordinate table, measured from P01 (South-East corner), and check both diagonals before the rig starts. ✅ **As installed: screw heads at -135mm** (15mm above the -150mm design). The ring beam goes on **10mm packers** (galvanised or stainless shim plates, full bracket seat) so its top lands at **Datum Zero (0mm)**; confirm the packing (or bracket height adjustment) with the contractor / engineer (MASTER_PLAN §1.2). Agree **corner cap plates** for the halved ring beam corners (P01, P03, P12, P14) with the contractor beforehand (MASTER_PLAN §1.2.2).
*   **Weekend 1:** Clear surface vegetation and loose debris. Set up a level storage area for deliveries: concrete-block bearers on polythene, tarps and tie-downs ready (MASTER_PLAN §1.2.3).

### Week 2: Retaining, Weed Control & Timber Joists
*   **Pit Perimeter, Geotextile & Rodent Mesh: Sequence.** Boards and geotextile go in **before the ring beam** (Saturday and Sunday of the week before Weekend 2), while the pit is open and the screw heads are the only obstructions. The rodent mesh goes on **after the ring beam is bolted** (Weekend 2), because its top edge fixes to the ring beam outer face. Materials: Jewson order in `research/suppliers/jewson-pit-perimeter-order.md`. The 6mm mesh and the line pins come from elsewhere.
    *   **As dug (user, 13 Oct 2026):** the **South and East** sides already have a ~400mm trench at -300mm (measured from the screw centres, so ~330mm clear of the ring beam). On the **South** side, concrete stands ~300mm from the screws (~230mm clear). Both are inside the 150–400mm clearance band, so **neither needs boarding**. The **North** end is oversized and stays unboarded too. **Only the West face gets retaining boards.**
    *   **Geometry:** ring beam outer face = **70mm outside the screw centre lines** (3408 − 3268 = 140mm). West board **inner face** = 200mm outside the ring beam = **270mm outside the P03–P07–P11–P14 line**. With the 22mm board and 47mm stake behind it, the **West earth face sits 339mm outside the screw line**.

*   **Saturday: Set Out, Trim & Board the West Face**
    1.  **Water check:** ✅ the pit stays dry, even after heavy rain (13 Oct 2026). Bail out any water from a fresh downpour and let the floor firm up before boarding.
    2.  **String the board line.** Run a string tight over the West screw centres (P03–P07–P11–P14). At both ends, measure **270mm square outward** with the 8m tape and drive a steel line pin into the ground past the North and South ends of the pit. Re-string on the pins. That string is the board inner face.
    3.  **Trim the West earth face** back to **69mm behind the string**, plumb, the full 300mm deep, along the whole West side and into the corners until it meets the oversized North end and the South trench. This is the **westward extension (~423mm)** if it isn't dug yet. Barrow the spoil well away from the edge.
    4.  **Check the floor** with the laser: **-300mm or deeper** everywhere, including the strip along the new West face. Rake out loose spoil, roots and sharp stones. Don't backfill low spots with clay.
    5.  **Measure the West run** at the board line. The pit is oversized at the North end, so the earth face may run past 5324mm. Board the full length of earth face that needs holding, and return a short board round a corner only if the clay there is crumbling.
    6.  **Drive the stakes** (47 × 50 × 600mm treated pointed pegs) tight against the trimmed earth face, plumb, wide face to the pit. Space them at **≤900mm centres**, with one 50mm in from each end and one under every board joint. Drive them with the club hammer until the tops are flush with the adjacent ground, **never above 0mm**; ~300mm of embedment below the -300mm floor. Saw off any that hit refusal and brush end-grain preservative on the cut top.
        *   **Over a buried slab** (no driving possible): let the continuous boards span the slab between stakes on soil either side (**≤1.2m** gap). If the slab is wider, stand a short stake on it, glue its foot with CT1 and wedge a dense block behind it against the earth face, or hire an SDS drill for 2 × Thunderbolt + a corner brace.
    7.  **Board the face:** two courses of **150 × 22mm** treated gravel board (300mm). The **bottom course sits on the pit floor**. The top course finishes flush with or just below the ground, **never above 0mm**, so the gravel margin can stay below the starter track. If the ground is lower than Datum Zero, rip the top course down to suit. **Cut plan (~5.4m run):** each course = one 3.6m board + one ~1.8m piece (one 3.0m board cut in half gives both courses' short pieces). **Put the short piece at the South end on one course and the North end on the other**, so the joints stagger ≥1.8m. Every joint lands on a stake.
    8.  **Fix:** 2 × SPAX A2 4 × 50mm per board per stake, driven from the pit side; pre-drill 3mm within 25mm of a board end. Check line against the string and plumb with the 1200mm level as you go. End-grain preservative on every cut.
    9.  **Backfill** the 47mm gap behind the boards between the stakes with **pea gravel** (~0.1m³, about 6 handy bags), rodded down in 100mm lifts. No clay backfill: it holds water against the timber.
    *   *Foreman Note: the unboarded South, East and North faces stay as dug. Don't undercut them; trim any overhang so the faces stand at or slightly back from vertical, and keep traffic off the edges.*

*   **Sunday: Geotextile (Zero-Build-Up, Weed & Moisture Control)**
    1.  **Measure the pit floor first**, earth face to earth face: ~3938mm East-West (270 West + 3268 + 400 East) and ~5.5m North-South (South concrete to the oversized North face). **Cut two pieces** off the 4.5 × 10m roll, each **floor width + ~870mm long**: 470mm on the West (300 up the boards + ~70 over the board/stake tops + 100 onto the ground) and 400mm on the East (300 up the earth face + 100 onto the ground). That is **~4.8m each** with the measurements above. Lay them **across** the pit (East-West), Piece A at the South end. Trim Piece B in width so it laps A by **300mm** at mid-length; keep the offcut for patches. **If either cross piece comes out over 5.0m, two won't fit the 10m roll: buy the Drivetex 11.1 × 4.5m roll instead (Jewson LSGDGF45).**
    2.  **Lay Piece A** loose over the floor, centred. At each of the screws in its zone, cut an **"X" just big enough to pass the bracket head**, push the membrane down over it to the floor and wrap the flaps round the shaft. Collar each with **two turns of 100mm Flashband** (half on the shaft, half on the membrane). Patch any oversized cut with an offcut laid underneath, lapped 150mm.
    3.  **Tension and peg:** pull it flat, then peg along the foot of every face at **600mm** with galvanised 100 × 150mm ground staples (Toolstation 79016). Peg the open field at ~1m on bare soil.
    4.  **Over the slabs:** no pegs. Run a **CT1 bead** under the membrane along every edge and seam that lands on concrete (~300mm dabs). The SDS + Thunderbolt + penny-washer method in MASTER_PLAN §1.1 also works if an SDS is to hand.
    5.  **Up the faces:**
        *   **West (boarded):** fold the membrane up the board face, over the top and onto the ground. At each stake, fix it just below the board top with **SPAX A2 4 × 50mm + A2 M6 penny washer**.
        *   **East and North (earth):** lay it up the earth face and ~100mm onto the ground, pegged at the top lip at **500mm**.
        *   **South (concrete):** run it to the foot of the concrete and ~100mm up its face, bonded with a CT1 bead.
        *   Fold the corners as "hospital corners" (folded, not cut). On the West, the ground lip ends **~250mm beyond the ring beam line** (150mm past the external cladding line). On the East, the 330mm trench already covers that. The future gravel margin covers the lips.
    6.  **Lay Piece B** the same way, lapping A by 300mm (B on top). Peg the lap at **300mm**, or CT1 it where it crosses a slab.
    7.  **Check:** no exposed soil anywhere in the pit, no loose flaps, every screw collar sealed, no gravel ballast (saves 28mm of heave void). Floor still -300mm or below.
    8.  **Lockdown:** nothing to protect from rain at this stage; the membrane is permeable.
*   **Weekend 2 — Ring Beam & Joists:**
    *   **Cut List (MASTER_PLAN §1.2.2):** 6 × 5.4m cut to 4924mm (East/West), 6 × 3.6m cut to 3408mm (South/North), 6 × 3.6m cut to 3408mm (internal beams, T-halved at both ends). Every ply runs full length, no butt joints.
    *   Build the 141x125mm ring beam and the two internal E-W beams: sash-clamp 3 lengths of 47x125mm C24 and laminate with **SPAX 8 × 140mm A2 countersunk T40 screws** (or the engineer-approved **Swiftfix 8 × 140mm A4** if SPAX can't deliver in time — trial two in an offcut, pre-drill if the outer ply splits). Alternating faces at 150mm centres (300mm per face): outer face 45mm from the top, inner face 45mm from the bottom, ≥100mm from cut ends, 2 extra screws each side of butt joints, butt joints only over screw positions (MASTER_PLAN §1.2 Lamination Screws).
    *   **Halved Corners:** On the laminated beams, cut a **141mm × 62.5mm** halving at each end — top half off the East/West beams, bottom half off the South/North beams (saw kerfs to depth, chisel clean). Drive the East/West inner-face lamination screws inside the halving now; access closes once the corner is assembled. Two coats of end-grain preservative on every cut face and cross-cut; let it dry. Dry-fit each corner and check it finishes flush at 0mm (±3mm) before fixing.
    *   Fit the 10mm packers on every bracket seat and level them to -125mm with a laser. Ring beam sits -125mm to 0mm; M10 bolt the East/West beams to the U-brackets. Drop the South/North beams onto the halvings and fix each corner with **4 × 8 × 100mm stainless structural screws** driven down, heads countersunk flush. Drop the internal beams into their T-halvings over P04/P07/P08/P11 (notch the East/West beams' top half first, same as the corners) and fix with 4 × 8 × 100mm screws each. Check both diagonals again.
    *   Hang 47x125mm joists (cut ~1448mm, one per bay) @ 400mm centres on **Simpson SAE250/47/2** face-fix hangers (101.5mm tall — never a taller hanger, it would stand proud of the deck) on the South/North ring beams and both faces of the internal beams, flush with the top (0mm). Screw the two edge joist lines tight to the East/West ring beam (8 × 100mm stainless, ~4 per piece). Double joists at heavy loads and intermediate screws. Flat 1200 × 30 × 5mm galvanised restraint straps on the **underside**, across the East/West ring beam and ≥3 joists at 600mm max centres, with solid strutting at each strap. Fix galvanised angle brackets (~40 × 40 × 3mm, 2 per bay) to the South/North ring beam inner faces between the hangers, horizontal leg flush at 0mm, to carry the deck edge. At the internal screw lines, fit twin joists (~130mm centres, each in its own hanger, bolted through 83mm packers). Joist undersides at -125mm give the 175mm heave void above the -300mm pit floor.
    *   *Foreman Note: Face-Fix hangers keep the ring beam top 100% timber, so no metal tabs puncture the DPC or pinch the sole plate.*
*   **Rodent Mesh (The 'L-Shape' Defense):** fit it once the ring beam is bolted and before the starter track goes on (Phase 3). Wear cut-resistant gloves: cut mesh edges are razor sharp.
    1.  **Cut strips:** split each 900mm-wide roll lengthwise into **two 450mm strips** with aviation snips, cutting tight against a wire so no long spikes are left. Two 6m rolls give 4 × 6m = 24m of strip for the ~16.7m perimeter + laps.
    2.  **Bend the "L":** clamp each strip between a ring beam offcut and the bench edge and fold it 90° along a wire line: **295mm vertical + 155mm flare**.
    3.  **Hang:** start at a corner. Top edge **flush with the ring beam top (0mm), never proud** (the DPC and sole plate land there). Fix with **SPAX A2 4 × 50mm + A2 M6 penny washers at 300mm**, in a row **50mm below the top** (clear of the starter track flange). This fixing is permanent; the starter track clamp is a second line of defence.
    4.  **Bracket cheeks and bolts** (10 perimeter screws): let the mesh lie over the U-bracket cheeks. Where a bolt head or nut stands proud, snip a slot just wide enough to pass it and close any gap over 6mm with an offcut patch screwed over it.
    5.  **Laps and corners:** overlap strips by **100mm**, screwed through both layers on the beam and pegged through both on the flare. At corners, cut the flare to a mitre, fold the vertical leg round with a 100mm return and screw it.
    6.  **Flare:** lay it flat on the geotextile, pointing **outward** (155mm, ~45mm short of the West boards and well clear of the South concrete and East face), pegged at **300mm** with the membrane pegs through mesh and membrane together.
    7.  **Test:** walk the perimeter with a 6mm drill bit. If it passes through anywhere (lap, corner, bracket, the gap between flare and floor), patch it.
*   **Insulation Support & Drop:** Mark a line 120mm below the joist tops (-120mm). Drive A4 stainless 5 × 60mm screws along it into every joist and beam side @ 300mm, leaving ~30mm proud as pegs. No timber battens (they would hang into the heave void). Cut the 120mm PIR tight, drop it onto the pegs flush with the joist tops, and seal all four edges of every board with **low-expansion** PU foam (Soudal Flexible, Toolstation; or Soudafoam Gun Grade, Screwfix). Notch the board top 3mm under each deck-edge bracket. Only open as many bays as you can deck or tarp that weekend.

### Week 3: The Weatherproof Sub-Deck
*   **Weekend 3:** Snap a line **135mm in from the ring beam outer face** all round: the deck stops there, leaving the 125mm sole plate zone + 10mm gap clear for NFS. Lay 22mm Caberdek P5 T&G chipboard (peel-off film, ~21kg solo lift) at 0mm, D4 glue in every T&G joint, SPAX WIROX 4.5 × 60mm screws at 300mm centres into joists and noggins. Leave the film on. This is the dry working platform for the superstructure (rated 42 days exposed).
*   **Sunday Lockdown (Weeks 2–3):** Move the 4 × 10m tarp from the (now used) timber stack to the platform: 4m across the width, surplus folded back at the ends, ridge over an offcut, tied down through every eyelet to blocks or sandbags. Timber and board stacks stay on bearers under their own tarps (MASTER_PLAN §1.2.3).

## 🗓️ Phase 2: Superstructure & Thermal Jacket (Weeks 4-6)
*New Forest SIPs build the shell. Hand them a perfect platform, check their work, and never leave bare OSB exposed once their wrap comes off.*

### Week 4 Prep: Platform Handover to New Forest SIPs
*   **Weeknights (Quiet, 45m-1.5h):** Check the platform before NFS arrive — the last time a mistake is cheap. Ring beam outer footprint **3408 × 4924mm**, diagonals equal, top level at **0mm (Datum Zero)** all round (±3mm). Deck screwed down and swept. Clear the delivery and handling route (Yarmouth ferry, shuttle vehicle for the final mile). Send NFS the targets: **SIP wall and sole plate outer faces flush with the ring beam outer face** (~16mm ledge inside), East/West top plates **+2216.7 / +2273.5mm** (wall panels **2216.7 / 2273.5mm** from the 0mm sole plate), 120mm roof oversail all round, and the factory-cut **270mm dMVHR core** position with spline clearance (MASTER_PLAN §4.2.1).

### Week 4: 🚨 BIG EVENT: NFS Shell Erection & Handover
*   **NFS on site:** DPC, sole plates, anchoring, wall and roof panels with all structural connections, wrapped in their **temporary breather membrane**.
*   **Handover Checklist (before they leave):**
    *   Walls plumb; outer faces flush with the ring beam; wall plan diagonals equal.
    *   Top plates: East **+2216.7mm**, West **+2273.5mm**; roof falls **1:60** to the East; **120mm** oversail on all four sides.
    *   Every opening (door, windows, clerestories, 270mm dMVHR core) in position and size — check against the MASTER_PLAN §1.0a opening schedule (panel-base heights).
    *   Wrap: laps shingled (upper over lower), taped closed over **every opening**, no loose flaps, roof shedding water at 1:60.
    *   Get from NFS: the membrane **product name and rated UV-exposure period**, and their fixing schedule.

### Weather Hold (Variable Length — Work Commitments)
*   The NFS wrap buys time, not unlimited time. 🚨 *Foreman Note: a breather membrane is not a roof; on a 1:60 roof, water sits on it. Roof PIR + EPDM is the first job after the hold. Over-sheet the roof with a tarp if the hold runs into wet or windy weeks.*
*   **Weekly walk-round (15 min):** lifted laps, wind damage, ponding on the roof; re-tape tears with Tescon Extora. After any named storm, check the same day.
*   **UV deadline:** count days from handover against the membrane's rated UV exposure. If it will overrun, sheet walls and roof with heavy-duty tarpaulins before the deadline.

### Week 5: 🚨 BIG EVENT: Roof Insulation & Weather-Tight Roof (one dry weekend)
*   **Forecast:** dry 2-day window only — the roof OSB is exposed once the wrap comes off.
*   **Saturday (Strip & Kerb):** Strip the NFS membrane from the **roof only**, in sections. Confirm the **1:60** fall across the short width. Tape the external roof panel joints. Fix the 80mm timber kerb batten on edge around the perimeter. If rain threatens, tarp the bare sections before stopping.
*   **Sunday (Weather-Tight):** Bond flat 80mm high-compressive PIR (Kingspan TR27) to the roof SIP's top OSB skin. Glue the 1.5mm EPDM onto the PIR.
*   **Sunday Lockdown:** Fit UPVC drip edge and kerb trims. The roof is permanently weather-tight. The NFS wrap stays on the walls until Week 6.

### Week 6: External Insulation, Starter Track & Tyvek Wrap
*   **Stripping Rule (One Face at a Time):** Strip the NFS membrane from **one elevation**, then insulate and Tyvek it before stopping. Never end a day with bare OSB on a wall. Start with the windward West face in good weather; leave sheltered faces for later weekends if needed.
*   **Ground Clearance (MASTER_PLAN §2.3B):** the base sits at 0mm (lawn level), so keep the finished gravel margin below 0mm (under the starter track) and falling away from the wall; nothing (lawn, soil, paving) bears against the XPS skirt.
*   **Starter Track & XPS Skirt:** Screw the **60mm Aluminium EWI Starter Track** into the sole plate / ring beam, level at **0mm** (base of the SIP wall), clamping the top edge of the rodent mesh behind it. Fix the **60mm x 180mm XPS Plinth Skirt** into the track (0mm to +180mm).
*   **Wood Fibre Insulation:** Fix **60mm Rigid Wood Fibre** to the SIP OSB from **+180mm** (on top of the XPS) with the manufacturer's **temporary insulation fixings** (plastic disc + screw, 2–4 per board) until the Week 8 battens clamp it. Seal the XPS/Wood Fibre joint with Compriband tape or vapour-permeable MS Polymer sealant.
*   **dMVHR Opening in the Wood Fibre:** Push a long drill bit out through the centre of the factory-cut 270mm SIP core to mark the axis outside. Cut a **270mm circle** through the Wood Fibre (handsaw, insulation knife or multi-tool). Tyvek over it; it is opened in Week 7.
*   **Breather Membrane:** Drape **Tyvek** over the Wood Fibre and XPS skirt and tape it to the front face of the starter track with Tescon Vana (*do NOT tuck into the track*). That elevation is now permanently protected. Repeat face by face until the NFS wrap is gone.

## 🗓️ Phase 3: Glazing, Cladding & Rainwater (Weeks 7-8)

### Week 7: AC Penetration, Glazing & Threshold Detailing
*   **AC Core Hole (Before Cladding):** Mount the indoor bracket for the Quick-Connect AC on the SIP OSB. Core ~65mm through the SIP and Wood Fibre at a 2-3° fall to the outside (condensate). Pass the pipe bundle out and leave it coiled. Foam the core gap airtight; seal inside with Tescon Vana / Roflex grommet.
*   **Service Cable Core Hole (Before Cladding):** Core ~32mm through the SIP and Wood Fibre behind the Consumer Unit, clear of the 1200mm spline joints, 1-2° fall to the outside. Seal the outside onto the Tyvek with a Pro Clima Kaflex grommet (or Tescon Extora tape). The electrician runs the SWA and Cat6a along the fence and dresses them in with a drip loop below the entry.
*   **dMVHR Wall Pipe (Before Cladding):** Cut the Zehnder Ø250mm wall pipe to the **296mm** finished wall at the unit (236mm wall + 60mm build-out; Zehnder template for exact projection). Cross-cut the Tyvek over the hole and fold back the flaps. Slide the pipe in at a **1° fall to the outside**, shim it level side-to-side in the 20mm annulus, and fill the SIP core and Wood Fibre gap with Illbruck FM330. Seal the Tyvek to the pipe with an **EPDM / Tescon Extora collar**. Cap both pipe ends. *Foreman Note: check the fall with a digital level before the foam cures — it can't be corrected afterwards.*
*   **Glazing & Door:** Install the six D-AC uPVC fixed windows and the ~1000mm × ≤ ~2060mm single-leaf (900mm leaf), half-glazed thermally broken aluminium door (double-glazed upper panel, insulated lower panel) in the factory-cut openings.
    *   *Door seals (MASTER_PLAN §2.7):* gaskets continuous and compressed on all four sides; threshold seal closes fully onto the flush +40mm threshold (no daylight with the door shut).
    *   *Door movement detail (MASTER_PLAN §2.7):* ~10mm jamb and ~15mm head gaps; jamb fixings only (packer behind each, nothing rigid through the head); expanding foam tape plus internal Contega with a movement fold at the head; never gun-foam alone. Record the opening diagonals and sill level in the site journal for re-checking after the first winter and summer.
    *   *Handling:* D-AC units weigh ~37 kg/m² of glass (a 1000x1000mm West unit is ~30 kg of glass alone). 2-person lift with heavy-duty suction cups, mandatory.
    *   *Expansion gap:* 10-12mm perimeter clearance around every frame (uPVC moves ~3× more than aluminium; a 1500mm clerestory moves ~4mm over a 40°C swing — follow the profile maker's guidance), sealed with pre-compressed expanding foam tape (Compriband / Illbruck illmod 600 / TP652) and low-expansion flexible PU foam, Pro Clima Tescon Vana inside.
    *   *Datums:* North/South/East clerestory tops at **1997mm FFL** (179.7mm head zone under the +2216.7mm plate); West windows (1000x1000mm) and door tops at **2080mm FFL** (153.5mm head zone under the +2273.5mm plate).
*   **Threshold & Drainage:** The SIP sole plate runs through the door opening and carries the frame. Check its top level across the opening: if it is above **+40mm FFL** (e.g., +47mm), plane / chisel it down to +40mm and brush-treat the cut surface with preservative before bedding the frame (MASTER_PLAN §2.7). Set an Aco HexDrain channel flush with the patio in front of the West door; apply **RIW Flexiseal** over the threshold junction.

### Week 8: Rainscreen Battens, Cladding & Rainwater System
*   **Rainscreen Battens:** **25x50mm treated battens @ 400mm centres**, vertical over the Tyvek, 150mm A4 EWI screws at alternating 60°/90° (Truss-Screw method). Staple **Perforated Bug Mesh** across the bottom 25mm gap (cavity inlet at 0mm). **Fit the ~18.6 lm top vent trim now:** Perforated Aluminium Vent Trim stapled to the roof SIP soffit across the 25mm batten gap (MASTER_PLAN §2.6 item 5). *Foreman Note: the cavity's only air outlet — it MUST go on before the top Hardie plank, which blocks access.*
*   **dMVHR Batten Frame:** Short 25mm batten noggins framing a **~280 × 280mm square** centred on the pipe, with gaps top and bottom so the cavity drains and ventilates.
*   **Cladding:** **James Hardie VL Fibre Cement Cladding** on the battens with Hardie™ Plank Screws (4.2x40mm T15 Torx), drill/driver on low torque (no impact drivers).
    *   *dMVHR notch:* The Zehnder outside panel (380 × 376mm) hides the opening, so cut a **~280 × 280mm square** across the planks that cross the pipe. Mark each plank in place, take it down, cut on trestles: long straight cuts with the fibre-cement circular saw blade (or score-and-snap), ~10mm relief hole at each inside corner, short cuts with a jigsaw (carbide-grit blade) or multi-tool. 🚨 *Silica: outdoors only, on-tool extraction, FFP3 mask.* Leave the outside wall panel off until the unit is hung (Week 12+).
*   **Rainwater Harvesting:** 112mm UPVC Deepflow guttering, Sure-Edge drip trims, downpipe diverter, 200L slimline water butt.

## 🗓️ Phase 4: Internal Thermal Floor & Passivhaus Detailing (Weeks 9-11)
*All work inside the dry, weather-tight building.*

### Week 9: Solid Timber Diaphragm Deck
*   **Birch Plywood Load-Spreader & Floor:** Peel the Caberdek film. Spread D4 structural adhesive and lay **18mm exterior birch plywood BB/BB** (2440 × 1220mm, square edge, ~36kg a sheet — two-person lift) over the 22mm Caberdek, joints staggered ≥300mm from the Caberdek joints, screwed into joists at 300mm centres — a solid 40mm laminated slab. Countersink the screws neatly; this face is the finished floor. Cover it with hardboard or Correx sheet for the rest of the fit-out.
*   **Utility Penetration:** Pull the SWA and Cat6a through the service core hole. Fill the SIP core void with Illbruck FM330 and seal the inside face to the SIP OSB with a Pro Clima Kaflex grommet / Tescon Vana tape before the Fermacell.

### Week 10: Internal Passivhaus Airtight Taping
*   **Airtight VCL Taping:** Tape every internal wall-to-wall, wall-to-roof and wall-to-floor joint, and all window and door reveals, with **Pro Clima Tescon Vana**. Tape the dMVHR pipe to the SIP OSB with overlapping Tescon Vana petals all round. This is the continuous airtight/vapour layer.

### Week 11: Direct-Mount Internal Boards & Floor Finish
*   **Direct-Mount Fermacell:** Pre-cut 15mm Fermacell, Jointstik on the edges, screwed to the taped SIP OSB with 30mm Fermacell screws (REI30/REI60 boundary fire rating).
*   **dMVHR Internal Build-Out:** With the main Fermacell up (cut neatly round the pipe), build the **60mm build-out**: 45 × 45mm planed treated timber frame screwed through the Fermacell into the SIP OSB, 40mm wood fibre infill, 15mm Fermacell face glued and screwed, boxed tight to the pipe. ≥50mm beyond the unit's mounting plate on every side (~600 × 700mm — Zehnder template). Finished wall at the pipe **≥275mm** (target 296mm).
*   **Floor Finish:** Lift the protection, vacuum, and apply clear hard-wax oil or floor lacquer to the birch plywood (FFL = +40mm). Do this after the dusty work.
