# SITE MANAGER'S WORKPLAN: Cowes High-Performance MgO Modular Workshop (4x5m)

**Status:** ACTIVE BUILD SCHEDULE  
**Foreman:** The DIY Project Coach  
**Target:** 20m² Passivhaus-Standard Envelope via Magnesium Oxide (MgO) SIPs  

---

## 🏗️ The Foreman's Rules of Engagement

Listen up. You’re a weekend warrior with a 9-5 job. Burnout, not budget, is the biggest threat to this build. If you blow out your back on a Tuesday evening, the project stalls for a month. We operate on three strict protocols:

1.  **The 'Big Event' Protocol:** For major milestones (Slab Pour, SIP Delivery, Roof Lift), you need a 48-hour countdown checklist. You don't do these alone. Get mates. Buy pizza.
2.  **Evening Task Triaging:** Cowes has neighbors. Tuesday nights are for 'Quiet/Low-Impact' tasks: taping the VCL, measuring, sealing splines, and tool maintenance. No chop saws after 7 PM.
3.  **The Sunday Lockdown:** At 4:00 PM every Sunday, tools go down. You execute a 'Weather-Tight & Secure' checklist to ensure the build stays dry and safe while you are at your desk on Monday morning.

---

## 🗓️ Phase 1: Groundworks & The Floating Raft (The Heavy Slog)
*This is the worst of the physical labor. Pace yourself.*

### Weekend 1: The Big Dig
*   **Saturday (Heavy):** Mark out the 4x5m footprint + 200mm extra all around for the French Drain. Excavate down ~250mm. You are hand-digging roughly 10 tonnes of earth. Hire a motorized wheelbarrow if the 30m path allows.
*   **Sunday (Heavy):** Lay the geotextile membrane. Wheelbarrow in 150mm of Type 1 MOT sub-base.
*   **Sunday Lockdown (4 PM):** Hire a vibrating compactor plate. Run 6 passes over the MOT. Cover the spoil heap with a tarp so it doesn't turn into a mudslide next week. Return the wacker plate.

### Weekend 2: The Tub & The Steel
*   **Saturday (Moderate):** Screed 25mm of blinding sand perfectly level. Assemble the 100-150mm Jackodur Atlas XPS perimeter L-elements and interlocking floor panels. *Foreman Note:* If this isn't level, the whole building will be crooked.
*   **Sunday (Moderate):** Loose-lay the 1200g DPM inside the XPS tub. Tape all seams with butyl tape. Drop in the 50mm plastic 'chairs' and lay the A193 Steel Mesh.
*   **Evening Task (Quiet):** Zip-tie the 16mm PEX-a Underfloor Heating pipe to the steel mesh at 150mm centers. Pressure test the loop with an air compressor and leave the gauge on.

### Weekend 3: 🚨 BIG EVENT: The Concrete Pour
*   **Saturday (All Hands):** The concrete lorry arrives. You need 2-3 people. Wheelbarrowing 2 cubic meters of concrete down a 30m path is brutal. You *must* brace the outside of the Jackodur XPS tub with timber pegs and gravel backfill, or the hydrostatic pressure of the wet concrete will blow the tub apart. Power-float or screed it level.
*   **Sunday (Rest):** Let the slab cure. Do nothing. Drink tea. 
*   **Sunday Lockdown:** Check the UFH pressure gauge. Cover the slab with a tarp if heavy rain or frost is forecast.

---

## 🗓️ Phase 2: The MgO SIPs Superstructure (The Fast Phase)
*This goes up incredibly fast. Measure twice, glue once.*

### Evening Prep: The Thermal Break
*   **Mid-Week (Quiet):** Snap chalk lines on the cured concrete. Bed the Compacfoam CF200 thermal break blocks onto the slab edge using a continuous, thick bead of CT1 polymer sealant (your capillary break). Drill and fix with stainless concrete anchors.

### Weekend 4: 🚨 BIG EVENT: Panel Delivery & Wall Assembly
*   **Friday (Delivery):** The MTL Ltd (Isle of Wight) delivery arrives. Carry the 150mm MgO wall panels down the 30m path. They weigh ~25-30kg each. Stack them flat on timber bearers, completely covered by a tarp.
*   **Saturday (High Focus):** Set the timber/SIP sole plate onto the Compacfoam. Stand the first corner panel. *Foreman Note:* Plumb it perfectly. If the corner is out by 2mm, the roof won't fit. 
*   **Sunday (Repetitive):** Join the rest of the wall panels using the insulated splines. Apply foaming structural PU adhesive to every joint before sliding them together. Drive the 100mm Spax structural screws to lock them tight.
*   **Sunday Lockdown:** The walls are up. Ensure the top continuous timber/MgO wall-plate is nailed down flat to tie the structure together. Check the weather forecast. Cover the open roof span with a massive heavy-duty tarp, tied down securely to the Compacfoam base.

### Weekend 5: 🚨 BIG EVENT: The Roof Lift
*   **Saturday (Heavy/Dangerous):** The 175mm MgO Roof SIPs are heavy and spanning 4 meters. Get 3 strong mates and two sturdy A-frame ladders. Slide the panels up onto the wall plates. Glue and screw the splines exactly as you did the walls. Ensure the 50cm overhang is positioned correctly.
*   **Sunday (Weather-Tight):** Tape all external roof joints. Apply the 1.5mm EPDM rubber membrane over the MgO roof deck using the water-based deck adhesive. Roll it flat to remove bubbles. 
*   **Sunday Lockdown:** Install the UPVC drip edge trims. The building is now officially in the "dry."

---

## 🗓️ Phase 3: Exterior Finish & Glazing (The Tidy Phase)

### Weekend 6: Windows, Doors & Drainage
*   **Saturday (Finesse):** Install the UPVC/Aluminium windows and doors into the rough openings. Use expanding foam around the frames.
*   **Sunday (Heavy):** Dig the perimeter trench and install the 112mm guttering. Hook the downpipe diverter to your 200L water butt, and route the overflow pipe to a soakaway 3m from the foundation. Backfill the French drain with 20mm washed gravel.

### Weekends 7 & 8: The Silicone Render
*   *Foreman Note:* Do not start this if rain is forecast within 24 hours.
*   **Saturday:** Apply the basecoat adhesive directly to the exterior MgO boards. Embed the fiberglass reinforcing mesh into the wet basecoat. Smooth it out. Let it cure.
*   **Following Saturday:** Prime the walls.
*   **Following Sunday:** Trowel on the self-colored thin-coat silicone render. Work on one full wall at a time to maintain a wet edge (prevents visible join lines). 

---

## 🗓️ Phase 4: Internal Passivhaus Detailing & Fit-Out
*Now you are working inside, out of the weather. Evening work is highly effective here.*

### Evening Tasks: The Airtight Envelope
*   **Weeknights (Quiet):** Tape every single internal panel joint, floor junction, and ceiling junction with **Pro Clima Tescon Vana** tape. Tape the window frames to the MgO boards using **Tescon Profil**. *Foreman Note:* Do not rush this. If you leave a pinhole, your Passivhaus airtightness fails.

### Weekend 9: The Service Cavity & Electrical Rough-In
*   **Saturday (Moderate):** Screw the 25x50mm treated timber battens horizontally at 400mm centers onto the internal MgO walls. 
*   **Sunday (Technical):** Pull all your electrical cabling (Twin & Earth) through the service cavity. Mount the airtight Kaiser socket boxes. Run the ducting for the dMVHR unit and core-drill the 100mm exhaust hole through the SIP. 

### Weekend 10: Internal Skin & Flooring
*   **Saturday (Heavy):** Screw the 15mm Fermacell boards to the service battens using 30mm Fermacell screws. Use Jointstik glue on the edges.
*   **Sunday (Finesse):** Prime the concrete slab. Hard-bond the engineered oak flooring directly to the concrete using SikaBond polyurethane adhesive. Leave a 10mm expansion gap at the perimeter.

### Final Evenings: Commissioning
*   **Weeknights (Quiet):** Paint the Fermacell. Mount the 1.2kW IR heating panel to the ceiling. Call the electrician to terminate the consumer unit, wire the Type C RCBOs, and issue the sign-off certificate. Hook up the 3kW ASHP to the UFH manifold outside.

## 🛑 The Foreman’s Final Warning
You are building a high-tech thermal envelope, not a shed. 
*   If the DPM tears, patch it immediately.
*   If you miss a glue line on a SIP spline, it will draft forever. 
*   Pace yourself. Eat well. Keep the site clean so you don't trip over a chop saw on a Sunday evening. 

Now get to work.
