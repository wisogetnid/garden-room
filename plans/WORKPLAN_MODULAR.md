# SITE MANAGER'S WORKPLAN: Cowes High-Performance MgO Modular Workshop (4x5m)

**Status:** ACTIVE BUILD SCHEDULE  
**Foreman:** The DIY Project Coach  
**Target:** 20m² Passivhaus-Standard Envelope via Magnesium Oxide (MgO) SIPs  

---

## 🏗️ The Foreman's Rules of Engagement

Listen up. You’re a weekend warrior with a 9-5 job. Burnout, not budget, is the biggest threat to this build. If you blow out your back on a Tuesday evening, the project stalls for a month. We operate on three strict protocols:

1.  **The 'Big Event' Protocol:** For major milestones (Slab Pour, SIP Delivery, Roof Lift), you need a 48-hour countdown checklist. You don't do these alone. Get mates. Buy pizza.
2.  **Evening Task Triaging:** Cowes has neighbors. Tuesday nights are for 'Quiet/Low-Impact' tasks: taping the VCL, measuring, sealing splines, and tool maintenance. No chop saws after 7 PM.
3.  **The Sunday Lockdown:** At 4:00 PM every Sunday, tools go down. You execute a 'Weather-Tight & Secure' checklist to ensure the build stays dry and safe while you are at your desk on Monday morning.

---

## 🗓️ Phase 1: Groundworks & The Floating Raft (The Heavy Slog)
*This is the worst of the physical labor. Pace yourself.*

### Weekend 1: The Big Dig
*   **Saturday (Heavy):** Mark out the 4x5m footprint + 200mm extra all around for the French Drain. Excavate down 150mm. You are hand-digging roughly 10 tonnes of earth. Hire a motorized wheelbarrow if the 30m path allows.
*   **Sunday (Heavy):** Lay the geotextile membrane. Wheelbarrow in 150mm of Type 1 MOT sub-base.
*   **Sunday Lockdown (4 PM):** Hire a vibrating compactor plate. Run 6 passes over the MOT. Cover the spoil heap with a tarp so it doesn't turn into a mudslide next week. Return the wacker plate.

### Weekend 2: The Tub & The Steel
*   **Saturday (Moderate):** Screed 25mm of blinding sand perfectly level. Assemble the 100mm Jackodur Atlas XPS perimeter L-elements and interlocking floor panels. *Foreman Note:* If this isn't level, the whole building will be crooked.
*   **Sunday (Moderate):** Loose-lay the 1200-gauge DPM inside the XPS tub. Tape all seams with butyl tape. Drop in the 50mm plastic 'chairs' and lay the A193 Steel Mesh.
*   **Evening Task (Quiet):** Zip-tie the 16mm PEX-a Underfloor Heating pipe to the steel mesh at 150mm centers. Pressure test the loop with an air compressor and leave the gauge on.

### Weekend 3: 🚨 BIG EVENT: The Concrete Pour
*   **Saturday (All Hands):** The concrete lorry arrives. You need 2-3 people. Wheelbarrowing 2.5 cubic meters of concrete down a 30m path is brutal. You *must* brace the outside of the Jackodur XPS tub with timber pegs and gravel backfill, or the hydrostatic pressure of the wet concrete will blow the tub apart. Power-float the min 100mm C25/30 mix level.
*   **Sunday (Rest):** Let the slab cure. Do nothing. Drink tea. 
*   **Sunday Lockdown:** Check the UFH pressure gauge. Cover the slab with a tarp if heavy rain or frost is forecast.

---

## 🗓️ Phase 2: The MgO SIPs Superstructure (The Fast Phase)
*This goes up incredibly fast. Measure twice, glue once.*

### Evening Prep: The Thermal Break
*   **Mid-Week (Quiet):** Paint liquid waterproof membrane (RIW Flexiseal) heavily over the XPS upstand. Snap chalk lines on the cured concrete. Bed the Compacfoam CF200 thermal break blocks onto the slab edge at the threshold. Set Aco HexDrain flush against the XPS upstand. 

### Weekend 4: 🚨 BIG EVENT: Panel Delivery & Wall Assembly
*   **Friday (Delivery):** The MgO SIP delivery arrives. *CRITICAL:* Inspect the panels. Ensure the factory has **pre-cut the openings for the dMVHR and the 2 standard windows (North and East elevations)** as specified. We do NOT want to be core-drilling on site and hitting structural splines. Carry the 150mm panels down the path and stack them flat on bearers under a tarp.
*   **Saturday (High Focus):** Secure the timber/MgO base plates to the concrete/Compacfoam using M12 resin anchors at 1200mm centers. Stand the first corner panel. *Foreman Note:* Plumb it perfectly. If the corner is out by 2mm, the roof won't fit. 
*   **Sunday (Repetitive):** Join the rest of the wall panels using the insulated splines. Apply foaming structural PU adhesive to every joint before sliding them together.
*   **Sunday Lockdown:** The walls are up. Ensure the top continuous wall-plate is secured into the top rebate. Cover the open roof span with a massive heavy-duty tarp, tied down securely.

### Weekend 5: 🚨 BIG EVENT: The Roof Lift
*   **Saturday (Heavy/Dangerous):** The 175mm MgO Roof SIPs are heavy and spanning 4 meters. *[OPEN RISK FLAG: Verify with the manufacturer if an intermediate Glulam beam is needed for this span before lifting!]* Get 3 strong mates and two sturdy A-frame ladders. Slide the panels up onto the wall plates. Glue and screw the splines exactly as you did the walls. Ensure the 50cm overhang is positioned correctly.
*   **Sunday (Weather-Tight):** Tape all external roof joints. Install tapered PIR insulation boards (1:60 fall). Apply the 1.5mm EPDM rubber membrane over the deck. Roll it flat to remove bubbles. 
*   **Sunday Lockdown:** Install the UPVC drip edge trims. The building is now officially "Weather-Tight".

---

## 🗓️ Phase 3: Exterior Finish & Glazing (The Tidy Phase)

### Weekend 6: Windows, Doors & Drainage
*   **Saturday (Finesse):** Install the UPVC/Aluminium windows and doors into the factory pre-cut openings. Seal with expanding foam and airtight tape.
*   **Sunday (Heavy):** Dig the 200mm French drain trench around non-door elevations. Install the guttering and downpipe diverter, routing it to your 200L water butt (no overflow to soakaway crate needed). Backfill the French drain with 20mm washed gravel.

### Weekends 7 & 8: The Silicone Render
*   *Foreman Note:* Do not start this if rain is forecast within 24 hours.
*   **Saturday:** Prime the walls.
*   **Sunday:** Trowel on the self-colored thin-coat silicone render directly to the external MgO boards. Work on one full wall at a time to maintain a wet edge. 

---

## 🗓️ Phase 4: Internal Passivhaus Detailing & Fit-Out
*Now you are working inside, out of the weather. Evening work is highly effective here.*

### Evening Tasks: The Airtight Envelope
*   **Weeknights (Quiet):** Tape every single internal panel joint, floor junction, and ceiling junction with **Pro Clima Tescon Vana** tape. Tape the window frames to the MgO boards. *Foreman Note:* Because the MgO boards are intrinsically airtight, this single taping step is all you need for the envelope. Do not rush it.

### Weekend 9: The Service Cavity & Electrical Rough-In
*   **Saturday (Moderate):** Screw the 25x50mm treated timber battens horizontally at 400mm centers onto the internal MgO walls. 
*   **Sunday (Technical):** Pull all your electrical cabling through the service cavity. Mount the airtight socket boxes. Install the dMVHR unit into its factory pre-cut opening. Run the rigid galvanized steel ducting for dust extraction. *CRITICAL SAFETY:* Install a Copper Earth Bonding strap to the galvanized ducting to ground it and prevent ATEX dust explosion risks.

### Weekend 10: Internal Skin & Flooring
*   **Saturday (Heavy):** Screw the 15mm Fermacell boards to the service battens using 30mm screws. Use Jointstik glue on the edges.
*   **Sunday (Finesse):** Apply breathable concrete sealer to the slab. Loose-lay the 7mm heavy-duty interlocking PVC tiles (e.g., Ecotile) directly over the concrete. No adhesive required. Leave a 5-10mm perimeter expansion gap.

### Final Evenings: Commissioning
*   **Weeknights (Quiet):** Paint the Fermacell. Mount the 1.2kW IR heating panel to the ceiling. Call the electrician to terminate the consumer unit (ensure 20A Type C RCBOs are used for induction motors), sign off the ATEX grounding, and issue the certificate. Hook up the 3kW ASHP to the UFH manifold outside. Set up your Table Saw and Planer on Sylomer® vibration decoupling pads.

You are building a high-tech thermal envelope, not a shed. 
*   If the DPM tears, patch it immediately.
*   If you miss a glue line on a SIP spline, it will draft forever. 
*   Pace yourself. Eat well. Keep the site clean so you don't trip over a chop saw on a Sunday evening. 

Now get to work.
