# Structural Validation: Foundation to Wall Connection (The 150mm Splash Zone)

**Agent:** @strategist
**Context:** The 150mm concrete slab is now poured *inside* the 150mm JACKODUR Atlas XPS upstands. Both the slab and the upstands are sitting entirely above the surrounding ground level, creating a perfect 150mm "Splash Zone" to protect the timber from ground moisture. 
**Question:** Does the structural envelope still need a continuous `Compacfoam CF200` block separating the timber sole plate from the concrete?

## Web Research & Passivhaus Thermal Bridge Analysis:
1. **The Role of the XPS Upstand:** In the JACKODUR Atlas system, the vertical XPS upstands act as permanent, insulated formwork. The concrete slab is poured inside them. Therefore, the perimeter edge of the concrete slab is already wrapped in 150mm of continuous high-performance XPS insulation.
2. **The "Warm Edge" Reality:** Because the concrete edge is fully insulated from the outside cold by the XPS upstand, the perimeter of the concrete slab is considered part of the *internal thermal envelope* (the "warm zone"). 
3. **The Timber Sole Plate:** Since the concrete perimeter is warm, any timber sole plate bolted directly to the top of the concrete (over a standard DPC membrane) will also remain warm. There is no thermal bridge for the heat to escape horizontally because the XPS upstand blocks it. The external 50mm Wood Fibre wall insulation drops down to overlap this XPS upstand, creating a perfect continuous thermal envelope ("Red Line" test passes).
4. **Is Compacfoam Required?** 
   * **Under the walls?** NO. Placing 50mm of expensive, high-density Compacfoam CF200 under the entire 18-meter perimeter of the walls is entirely redundant and unnecessarily complicated. The treated timber sole plate can sit directly on the DPC on the concrete slab.
   * **At the Door Threshold?** YES. To install a heavy lift-and-slide door or asymmetric 1.5 door at floor level, the XPS upstand must be cut away at the doorway. Removing the XPS upstand breaks the thermal envelope. Here, a block of Compacfoam CF200 (~2m long) must be bedded onto the concrete to support the heavy door frame while restoring the thermal break exactly at the threshold.

## Conclusion & Action Plan:
The 150mm splash zone and XPS upstand geometry completely eliminates the need for a continuous Compacfoam thermal break under the walls. This simplifies the build and saves roughly £500 in materials.

1. **Architect Action:** Update `MASTER_PLAN_SIPS.md` to restrict Compacfoam CF200 *only* to the door threshold (~2 linear meters). Specify that standard 140x38mm treated timber sole plates sit directly on the DPC-covered concrete for the rest of the perimeter.
2. **Foreman Action:** Update `WORKPLAN_SIPS.md` to reflect this massive simplification. The weekend warrior no longer has to perfectly bed and level 18 meters of Compacfoam before standing the walls!
