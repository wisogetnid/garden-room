# **Comparative Analysis of Advanced Structural Insulated Panel Strategies for High-Performance Garden Architecture**

The construction of high-performance garden rooms in the United Kingdom has undergone a fundamental shift toward Modern Methods of Construction (MMC), primarily driven by the need for superior thermal efficiency, rapid on-site assembly, and compliance with increasingly stringent Building Regulations. The transition from traditional timber-frame solutions to Structural Insulated Panels (SIPs) offers a paradigm shift in how the building envelope is conceptualized. Unlike a conventional frame where insulation is an infill, a SIP system utilizes the insulation core as a structural component, creating a "stressed-skin" assembly that outperforms traditional methods in airtightness, thermal bridging, and structural rigidity.1 This report provides an exhaustive technical analysis of wooden SIP strategies, focusing on their application in Passivhaus-aligned garden rooms, their fire safety performance in boundary conditions, and their structural validation when integrated with insulated floating raft foundations.

## **Structural Mechanics and Material Composition of Modern SIPs**

The engineering of a structural insulated panel is rooted in the composite behavior of its constituent parts. A SIP functions similarly to a wide-flange steel I-beam; the rigid outer facings (the "skins") act as the flanges, resisting compression and tension, while the internal insulation core acts as the web, providing shear resistance and maintaining the distance between the skins to prevent buckling.3 For the garden room context, the selection of facing and core materials determines not only the energy profile of the building but also its lifespan and regulatory compliance.

### **Performance Characteristics of Structural Facings**

In the UK residential and garden building sector, Oriented Strand Board Grade 3 (OSB/3) is the predominant facing material. OSB/3 is a moisture-resistant, structural-grade board manufactured from wood strands cross-oriented and bonded with formaldehyde-free synthetic resins under high heat and pressure.4 The manufacturing process involves several critical stages: log preparation, stranding, drying to a moisture content of 5-10%, blending with resins and waxes, and finally pressing at approximately 600 PSI.6 This precision results in a board with consistent structural properties and a thermal conductivity ($\\lambda$) of approximately $0.13 W/mK$.5  
Alternative facing materials, such as Plywood and Magnesium Oxide (MgO) boards, are specified for specialized applications. Plywood offers high lateral strength but is often limited by price and availability in the large formats required for SIP production.3 MgO boards have gained traction due to their mineral-based, non-combustible nature (Euroclass A1), making them highly suitable for garden buildings situated within one meter of a property boundary.7

| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **OSB/3** | High racking/axial load capacity | Euroclass D (Combustible) | Moisture-resistant (Class 3\) | Standard residential/offices |

### **The Insulation Core: Thermal Physics and Material Selection**

The insulation core is the primary determinant of the panel’s thermal resistance ($R$-value). While Expanded Polystyrene (EPS) and Polyurethane (PUR) dominate the current market, the choice is influenced by the desired balance between panel thickness, cost, and environmental impact.3  
Expanded Polystyrene (EPS) cores are typically pressure-laminated to the OSB facings using high-strength adhesives. EPS is a closed-cell material that does not deteriorate over time, as it does not rely on blowing agents that can leak.4 Modern "carbon-treated" or graphite EPS offers improved thermal performance over standard white EPS.10 Polyurethane (PUR) and Polyisocyanurate (PIR) cores, however, are often injected into the cavity between facings under high pressure. The foam expands and cures, creating an autogenous bond with a force of approximately 50 lbs per square inch.3  
A critical emerging strategy for Passivhaus garden rooms is the use of wood fiber cores. Wood fiber provides a "vapor-open" (breathable) system, which is essential for managing moisture in buildings without a heavy reliance on plastic membranes.13 Wood fiber also possesses a higher specific heat capacity than synthetic foams, providing superior "decrement delay"—the time taken for external temperature fluctuations to penetrate the building envelope—which is vital for preventing summer overheating in highly glazed garden rooms.13

| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **EPS** | $0.030 \- 0.038 W/mK$ | Low | 100% Recyclable | Long-term stable $R$-value |
| **PUR/PIR** | $0.022 \- 0.024 W/mK$ | Medium | High embodied energy | Thinnest wall profiles |
| **Wood Fiber** | $0.038 \- 0.042 W/mK$ | High | Renewable/Carbon-negative | Thermal mass; breathability |

## **Passivhaus Performance Metrics for SIP Garden Rooms**

The Passivhaus standard requires an extremely low heating demand, typically achieved through ultra-low U-values, exceptional airtightness, and the elimination of thermal bridges. SIPs are naturally aligned with these goals because of their factory-controlled manufacturing and continuous insulation layers.2

### **Calculating Thermal Transmittance (U-values)**

To meet Passivhaus standards in the UK, wall U-values generally need to be between $0.10 W/m^2K$ and $0.15 W/m^2K$. The calculation for the U-value of a SIP panel is:

$$U \= \\frac{1}{R\_{si} \+ \\sum \\frac{d}{\\lambda} \+ R\_{se}}$$  
Where $R\_{si}$ and $R\_{se}$ are internal and external surface resistances, $d$ is the thickness of each layer, and $\\lambda$ is the thermal conductivity of the material.

| SIP Thickness (mm) | Core Material | U-Value (W/m2K) | Passivhaus Suitability |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **122 mm** | PIR (100mm core) | 0.209 | Needs additional insulation wrap |
| **142 mm** | PIR (120mm core) | 0.179 | High-performance standard |
| **172 mm** | PIR (150mm core) | 0.144 | **Ideal for walls** |
| **200 mm** | EPS (178mm core) | 0.150 | Standard eco-build target |
| **222 mm** | EPS (200mm core) | 0.110 | Premium/Near-passive |

While PIR panels achieve lower U-values at thinner profiles, EPS systems like the "Eco Premium" use deeper panels (up to 222mm) to reach $0.11 W/m^2K$, which significantly exceeds the current Part L Building Regulation of $0.18 W/m^2K$.16

### **Airtightness and Joint Integrity**

Airtightness is perhaps the most significant challenge in garden room construction, where small volumes make heat loss through infiltration disproportionately high. In traditional timber frames, airtightness relies on membranes that are prone to puncture during electrical installation. SIPs, however, use the OSB facings themselves as the air barrier.1  
The primary risk to airtightness lies in the joints between panels. Professional SIP strategies utilize insulated splines—miniature SIP strips inserted into rebates in the panel edges—which are then sealed with structural adhesive or expanding foam. This method ensures a continuous foam-to-foam connection, eliminating the convective heat loss found in traditional stud-work.3 Expert SIP builders typically achieve airtightness levels of $\\leq 1.0 m^3/h \\cdot m^2$ @ 50Pa, which is the threshold for Passivhaus certification and significantly better than the typical timber frame result of $3-5 m^3/h \\cdot m^2$.17

### **Thermal Bridging and Linear Heat Loss ($\\psi$-values)**

Thermal bridging occurs where a highly conductive material penetrates the insulation layer. In timber frames, every stud is a thermal bridge. SIPs minimize this by reducing the frequency of timber members within the walls and roofs.20 Junctions such as corners and eaves are meticulously detailed with single-piece corner panels or thermal-break splines to prevent "cold spots" that can lead to condensation and mold growth.12 Thermal bridge-free design is achieved when the linear thermal transmittance ($\\psi$-value) is $\\leq 0.01 W/mK$.23

## **Fire Safety Engineering and Boundary Proximity Regulations**

Fire safety is a primary regulatory driver for garden rooms, particularly those exceeding 15 square meters or situated within one meter of a property boundary.24 Compliance with Approved Document B (Fire Safety) is mandatory for these structures to prevent external fire spread between properties.27

### **Boundary Conditions and Material Non-Combustibility**

For garden buildings between 15 $m^2$ and 30 $m^2$ located within 1 meter of a boundary, the structure must be constructed "substantially from non-combustible materials".25

1. **Standard SIP Limitations:** Traditional OSB-faced SIPs with EPS or PUR cores are classified as combustible (Euroclass D or E). To be used near a boundary, they must be enhanced with non-combustible cladding systems.31  
2. **MgO SIP Advantage:** Magnesium Oxide (MgO) SIPs are inherently non-combustible (Euroclass A1/A) and can resist fire for up to 4 hours. They do not emit toxic smoke when exposed to heat, making them the superior choice for boundary wall construction.7  
3. **Sleeping Accommodation:** If the garden room is used as a granny annexe or guest suite, it must meet full fire regulations regardless of size. This includes 30-to-60-minute fire resistance ratings (REI 30/60) for walls and floors, mains-powered smoke detection, and egress-compliant windows.25

### **Validated Fire Performance of SIP Assemblies**

Technical data from manufacturers like Hemsec demonstrates that through-wall SIP assemblies can achieve significant fire resistance when combined with appropriate internal linings.5

| Assembly Type | Internal Lining | External Finish | Fire Rating |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Standard SIP (142mm)** | 12.5mm Type A Plasterboard | Timber Cladding | 30 Minutes (REI 30\) |
| **Enhanced SIP (142mm)** | 15mm Knauf Fire Panel | Render on Carrier Board | 60 Minutes (REI 60\) |
| **MgO SIP (100mm)** | Plaster Finish | Direct Render | 120+ Minutes |

Data from fire tests (e.g., Report EUI-24-000066) shows that a 182mm loadbearing SIP wall with 15mm Knauf Fire Panel and timber battens can sustain a 40kN load for 66 minutes without failure in integrity, insulation, or loadbearing capacity.33 This confirms that SIPs are a viable and safe alternative to masonry or traditional timber frames in high-risk locations.

## **Structural Validation of SIPs with Insulated Raft Foundations**

The integration of a SIP structure with a "floating" or "insulated" raft foundation is a critical requirement for Passivhaus builds. This system ensures the thermal envelope is continuous under the building, eliminating the massive thermal bridge typical of strip or trench foundations.34

### **Mechanics of the Floating Raft System**

A floating raft foundation, such as the Jackodur Atlas system, utilizes high-density Extruded Polystyrene (XPS) as both the insulation layer and the permanent formwork for the concrete slab.35 XPS is specified because of its high compressive strength (300–700 kPa) and its closed-cell structure, which makes it impervious to moisture and ground-water.35

| Component | Specification | Function |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Sub-Base** | 150mm Compacted MOT Type 1 | Primary load-bearing layer |
| **Insulation Layer** | 200mm+ XPS (Jackodur Atlas) | Thermal separation; formwork |
| **Moisture Barrier** | 1200 Gauge DPM | Damp/Radon protection |
| **Slab** | 250mm Reinforced Concrete | Structural mass; load distribution |
| **Edge Insulation** | 100mm Vertical XPS Upstand | Thermal bridge elimination at edge |

Validation of this system for SIPs requires ensuring that the vertical loads from the SIP walls—which are bearing walls—are transferred effectively into the reinforced slab and then evenly distributed across the XPS insulation. The Jackodur Atlas BBA certificate confirms its suitability for timber frame and SIP loads, provided the stress on the XPS at the serviceability limit state (SLS) does not exceed 83 kPa.37

### **The SIP-to-Foundation Junction Detail**

The interface between the SIP wall and the raft foundation is where airtightness and thermal performance are either won or lost. The traditional method of placing a wall on a platform floor is discouraged in Passivhaus builds due to air leakage risks.38

1. **The Sole Plate Interface:** A pressure-treated timber sole plate (sill plate) is fixed directly to the concrete slab using anchor bolts. A damp-proof course (DPC) must be placed under the sole plate to act as a capillary break, preventing ground moisture from "wicking" into the OSB facings.38  
2. **Precise Alignment:** The sole plate must be positioned with extreme accuracy (typically \+/- 5mm). It is often set 7/16-inch (11mm) from the edge of the slab upstand to allow the outer OSB skin of the SIP panel to bear directly on the structural base.39  
3. **Continuous Edge Insulation:** The 100mm perimeter insulation surround of the raft should overlap or butt tightly against the SIP wall insulation. This ensures that the heat-loss path from the slab to the outside air is entirely blocked by at least 100mm of high-performance insulation.35  
4. **Airtightness Sealing:** The junction is sealed using a combination of expanding foam (between the panel base and the sole plate) and specialized airtightness tape or membranes on the internal face. An expansion joint is typically maintained between the internal floor screed and the SIP sole plate to prevent cracking and maintain the air seal over time.3

## **Material Strategies: Synthetic vs. Wood Fiber Cores**

For the garden room context, the choice between synthetic (PIR/EPS) and bio-based (Wood Fiber) SIPs involves a trade-off between thermal thickness and environmental philosophy.

### **Synthetic Core SIPs (PIR/EPS)**

Synthetic cores offer the highest thermal performance per millimeter of thickness. For a garden room where space is often limited by planning rules (e.g., the 2.5m height limit for buildings within 2m of a boundary), the ability to use a 122mm or 142mm PIR panel to reach a high U-value is a significant advantage.17 Furthermore, synthetic foams are resistant to moisture and mold, providing a robust solution for the damp UK climate.3

* **PIR:** Best for maximizing internal floor area; achieves $0.18 W/m^2K$ with a 142mm panel.17  
* **EPS:** Best for long-term stability and cost-effectiveness; achieves $0.15 W/m^2K$ with a 200mm panel.5

### **Wood Fiber and "Breathable" SIP Strategies**

A "vapor-open" or "breathable" SIP replaces the internal foam with wood fiber and the OSB with vapor-permeable sheathing like Panelvent or Hunton Bitroc.14 This strategy is increasingly favored for Passivhaus builds that prioritize indoor air quality and sustainability.

* **Hygroscopic Buffering:** Wood fiber can absorb and release moisture, helping to regulate internal humidity levels naturally.13  
* **Acoustic Insulation:** Due to its higher raw density and porous structure, wood fiber SIPs provide superior sound absorption coefficient ratings, which is critical for garden offices in busy urban areas.13  
* **Carbon Sequestration:** Wood fiber SIPs lock up CO2 for the life of the building (60–100 years), offering a much lower embodied carbon profile than synthetic foams.14

| Feature | PIR/EPS SIP | Wood Fiber SIP |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Wall Thickness** | Thin (122-172mm) | Thick (250-300mm+) |
| **Vapor Status** | Vapor Closed | Vapor Open (Breathable) |
| **Decrement Delay** | Low (Quick heat gain) | High (Stable temp) |
| **Acoustics** | Good (System-dependent) | Excellent (High density) |
| **Cost** | Competitive | Higher (Premium system) |

3. **Hemsec (National):** One of the UK's largest BBA-certified manufacturers. Their timber SIPs are a benchmark for residential and garden room quality, offering a 60-year lifespan and proven fire test results (REI 30/60).33  
4. **Direct Sips (National):** Provides customizable kits for DIY or trade installation, including "Sanctuary" and "Deluxe" garden office ranges that utilize PIR-core panels for maximum efficiency.45

### **Implementation and Quality Assurance Protocols**

The failure of high-performance garden rooms often stems from poor execution rather than material deficiency. The Structural Timber Association (STA) provides a "SIP Pocket Guide" that outlines the gold standard for installation.33

* **Pre-Arrival Verification:** The sub-structure ( raft foundation) must be verified as level and square before delivery. Any discrepancy beyond 5mm will cause compounding errors as the panel walls grow.3  
* **Vertical Chase Management:** Electrical and HVAC chases must be pre-planned. Cutting new chases into SIPs on-site can compromise structural integrity and thermal continuity.21  
* **Vapor Control Continuity:** In synthetic SIPs, the internal OSB layer acts as the vapor control layer (VCL). Continuity must be maintained at all junctions with specialized SIP tape. If warm, humid air reaches the outer cold sheathing, it can cause interstitial condensation and rot.3

## **Comparative Analysis of Sustainability and Lifecycle**

The choice of SIP strategy has profound implications for the building’s lifecycle carbon footprint. Timber and wood fiber lead on upfront "embodied" carbon, while synthetic SIPs excel in reducing "operational" carbon through ultra-tight envelopes.18

### **Carbon Payback and Operational Energy**

Studies indicate that SIP buildings can be 50% more energy efficient than traditional timber frames.2 This efficiency translates to a "carbon payback" period where the energy saved during the building’s operation offsets the higher embodied carbon of the synthetic insulation.18

* **Timber Frame Typical:** $0.15 \- 0.20 W/m^2K$; Airtightness $3-5 m^3/h \\cdot m^2$.  
* **SIP Panel Typical:** $0.10 \- 0.17 W/m^2K$; Airtightness $\< 1.0 m^3/h \\cdot m^2$.

The reduction in heating and cooling demand—often 20-50% lower than conventional builds—compounds over the 60-year life of the structure, making SIPs a critical tool for achieving Net Zero carbon targets.18

## **Validation Conclusion: The Integrated Garden Room Strategy**

Based on the exhaustive analysis of available materials and structural strategies, the replacement of a timber-frame solution with a SIP envelope in the described garden room context is not only feasible but technically superior for Passivhaus performance.

### **Optimal Configuration for the Master Plan**

To maximize internal space while meeting Passivhaus and fire safety standards, the following strategy is validated:

1. **Wall Structure:** Use 172mm PIR-core SIPs (150mm core) for the primary envelope to achieve a U-value of $0.14 W/m^2K$. For walls within 1 meter of a boundary, MgO-faced panels should be substituted to provide Euroclass A1 non-combustibility and a fire rating of up to 4 hours.7  
2. **Foundation Interface:** The floating raft insulation (Jackodur Atlas or equivalent) is perfectly compatible with SIP bearing walls. The 100mm perimeter insulation surround must be aligned with the SIP insulation core to maintain thermal bridge continuity. The SIP sole plate must be isolated from the slab by a DPC and sealed internally for airtightness.35  
3. **Airtightness:** Utilize an insulated spline joining system with factory-cut rebates. Supplement the internal panel joints with high-performance vapor-permeable or airtightness tape to ensure the structure meets the $\\leq 0.6$ ACH threshold required for Passivhaus certification.15  
4. **Sustainability:** If the Master Plan prioritizes low embodied carbon over maximum internal space, a wood fiber-based closed panel system (e.g., Buildakit) using Panelvent sheathing should be adopted. This will require a total wall thickness of approximately 300mm to match the thermal performance of a 172mm PIR SIP but will provide superior acoustic damping and a carbon-negative envelope.14

The technical evidence confirms that the integration of wooden SIPs with an insulated floating raft foundation represents the pinnacle of modern garden room engineering, offering a durable, fire-safe, and ultra-low-energy environment that fulfills all requirements of the Master Plan..4
