# **Technical Analysis of Jackodur Atlas Foundation Systems: Supplier Landscape and Logistical Optimization for the Isle of Wight and Southern United Kingdom**

The construction of high-performance building envelopes in the contemporary regulatory environment requires a sophisticated approach to sub-structure thermal management. In the United Kingdom, the shift toward the Future Homes Standard and the increasing prevalence of Passivhaus-certified designs have elevated the importance of foundation systems that eliminate thermal bridging while providing structural stability. The Jackodur Atlas system, a proprietary solution developed by Jackon Insulation and now part of the international BEWI Group, represents a market-leading integration of formwork and extruded polystyrene (XPS) insulation.1 This report provides an exhaustive evaluation of the supplier infrastructure supporting the Jackodur Atlas system, specifically focused on the logistical constraints of the Isle of Wight and the broader Southern UK coastal region. The analysis examines the intersection of material science, maritime logistics, and the specific needs of the self-builder market for small-scale projects such as premium garden rooms.

## **The Structural and Thermal Evolution of the Jackodur Atlas System**

The Jackodur Atlas foundation system is founded upon the chemical and mechanical advantages of extruded polystyrene (XPS). Since the acquisition of Jackon Insulation by the BEWI Group in October 2022, the system has been positioned within a larger framework of sustainability and circular economy principles, ensuring that the production of raw materials and the end-of-life recycling of the components align with modern environmental mandates.1 The system is fundamentally designed to provide a continuous, high-performance thermal break between the building's floor slab and the ground, utilizing an innovative interconnection system that facilitates rapid installation without the necessity of traditional timber shuttering.3  
For the self-builder or the developer of a small-scale garden room, the choice of XPS over expanded polystyrene (EPS) is often dictated by the requirement for moisture resistance and long-term compressive strength. XPS is manufactured through a process of melting polystyrene beads and injecting carbon dioxide, resulting in a closed-cell structure that is virtually impervious to water.2 Data indicates that while standard EPS can absorb up to 10% moisture over its lifespan—leading to a potential 50% degradation in its insulative properties—XPS from the BEWI Jackodur range maintains water absorption below 1% even when subjected to high pressure or frequent freeze-thaw cycles.2 This characteristic is of paramount importance for construction on the Isle of Wight, where coastal humidity and fluctuating water tables can compromise the integrity of less robust materials.

For lightweight garden rooms, the Atlas 300 grade is the industry standard, whereas more substantial residential projects may require the Atlas 500 or 700 grades to satisfy the Serviceability Limit State (SLS) requirements.4

## **Proximity and Geographic Distribution: Mapping Suppliers Near the Isle of Wight**

Logistical efficiency for an Isle of Wight project is heavily dependent on the proximity of the supplier to the key ferry terminals in Southampton, Portsmouth, and Lymington. The cost of maritime transport often scales with the distance traveled on the mainland, making a "closer is better" strategy essential for maintaining project budgets.

## **Renders World / Renders Direct (Southampton)**

Situated in Southampton, Renders World represents the most geographically proximal official distributor for Atlas products.9 As a premier builders’ superstore with a specialized focus on External Wall Insulation (EWI) and foundation systems, they are uniquely positioned to serve the Isle of Wight market via the Red Funnel ferry route.9 Their specialization in XPS foundation boards—ranging from $10 mm$ to $200 mm$ in thickness—allows for immediate availability of components that might otherwise require long lead times from general merchants.9  
The reliability of Renders World is underscored by their 4.7 Trustpilot rating, with specific customer feedback citing swift stock verification and rapid dispatch.11 For the self-builder, their ability to provide technical support for both trade and homeowners is a significant advantage, particularly when navigating the complexities of a first-time insulated raft foundation installation.9

## **Civils Store (Southern Branch)**

The Civils Store operates as an independent, expert supplier of civil engineering and groundworks materials, with a dedicated Southern depot located at Area 41, The Vinery, Poling, West Sussex (BN18 9PY).13 While slightly further from the immediate Southampton ferry terminals than Renders World, their location remains highly accessible for the Portsmouth (Wightlink) ferry route, which serves Fishbourne on the Isle of Wight.  
Civils Store is a recognized partner for BEWI and stocks the Jackodur Atlas system.1 Their reliability is reinforced by their recognition as the "Civils Merchant of the Year" at the 2024 Builders Merchants Awards.13 A unique aspect of their business model is the profit-sharing scheme, where 20% of profits are shared with all employees every three months; the company suggests this fosters a higher level of customer service and technical investment from their staff.13 This "favored supplier" ethos is particularly relevant for small projects where personalized service can prevent costly ordering errors.

## **Johnson’s Insulation Supplies (Eastleigh and West Sussex)**

Johnson’s Insulation Supplies has provided thermal, acoustic, and fire protection materials across the South since 1984\.15 Their Eastleigh depot (SO50 6RS) is located just north of Southampton, offering an excellent geographic compromise for projects sourcing materials for the island.

## **ICF Southern (West Sussex)**

Based in West Sussex, ICF Southern specializes in the supply of insulated concrete formwork and associated materials.16 While they are primarily recognized as authorized distributors for Nudura products, their extensive experience in the Southern UK construction market—including specific projects on the Isle of Wight—makes them a valuable technical resource.16 They provide not only materials but also specialized training through their ICF Training Academy, which offers intensive courses in installation techniques for various ICF-adjacent systems.16

## **Economic Benefits of the "Zero-Waste" System**

One of the most profound third-order effects of the Atlas system for a small project is the mitigation of waste disposal costs. On the Isle of Wight, skip hire and construction waste management are expensive due to the necessity of transporting waste back to the mainland for processing. The Atlas system is supplied pre-cut and numbered to match the specific floor plan of the garden room, ensuring that only the necessary materials are delivered to the site.4 This "precision-cut" approach not only saves on material costs but also reduces the physical footprint of the construction site, a critical factor for small residential gardens where space is at a premium.

## **Reliability, Trust, and Professional Validation**

In the construction sector, reliability is measured through third-party certification and institutional acceptance. The Jackodur Atlas system possesses several key credentials that offer security to the self-builder.

## **BBA and NHBC Acceptance**

The system is covered by BBA Certificate 20/5812, which validates its performance in the UK's unique climatic and regulatory environment.2 Furthermore, the BEWI Jackodur Atlas system has achieved NHBC Acceptance, which simplifies the insurance and warranty process for residential builds.2 This is a major point of difference compared to some generic raft systems, as it provides construction companies and individual builders with a heightened level of confidence during the specification phase.28

## **Performance in Sustainable Construction**

The Atlas system's reliability in high-performance builds is demonstrated by its certification by the Passive House Institute.3 This certification indicates that the system has been rigorously tested for its ability to eliminate thermal bridges, a critical requirement for achieving "A" ratings in energy efficiency.8 For the garden room project, this means the foundation will perform consistently over its 50-year design life without the "thermal sagging" or moisture ingress that can plague traditional concrete-on-EPS foundations.2

## **Support for the Self-Builder: Navigating the Small Project**

The self-builder often lacks the extensive plant equipment and multi-disciplinary teams of large contractors. The Jackodur Atlas system is engineered to mitigate these gaps through its "simple inter-connection system".3

## **Pre-Cut Customization and Site Management**

For a garden room, the "JRS System" (standard size elements) or the "Full Bespoke System" can be selected based on the complexity of the footprint.31 The full bespoke system is particularly well-suited for self-builders as it includes a custom installation plan and pre-cut parts that require no specialized tools for assembly.4 The "tongue-and-groove" joints act as a guide, reducing the margin for error during the initial layout phase.3

## **Technical Support Infrastructure**

BEWI and its distributors provide a comprehensive support network, including:

1. **Training Manuals:** Detailed modules covering site preparation, rebar installation, and concrete pouring.31  
2. **Dedicated Sales Teams:** National experts available for technical presentations and specific advice for individual self-build projects.32  
3. **On-Site Practical Support:** Many specialist suppliers, including ICF Building Solutions, offer turn-key solutions that include on-site guidance to ensure the integrity of the concrete pour.5

## **Conclusion: Strategic Supplier Selection for the Isle of Wight**

For the construction of an insulated garden room on the Isle of Wight, the optimal supplier choice is a function of logistical proximity and the depth of technical support.  
**Renders World (Southampton)** emerges as the primary recommendation for immediate proximity and established reliability in the Southern region. Their location significantly minimizes mainland haulage and maximizes the efficiency of the Red Funnel ferry crossing. Their expertise in Atlas products and high customer satisfaction ratings suggest a high degree of trust for the small-scale builder.9  
**Civils Store (Southern)** represents the best choice for those requiring a broad range of groundworks materials beyond just the insulation system. Their "Merchant of the Year" status and expert local knowledge provide a safety net for self-builders navigating the complexities of civil engineering.13  
**ICF Building Solutions** remains the premier choice for the self-builder who requires a "hand-holding" approach. Despite their northern base, their turn-key design and engineering support for the Jackodur Atlas raft system can be a decisive factor in the success of a first-time build, potentially offsetting the higher logistical costs through the prevention of on-site errors.5  
The implementation of the Jackodur Atlas system on the Isle of Wight is a technically sound and economically defensible strategy. By integrating thermal insulation and formwork into a single, moisture-resistant XPS system, the builder ensures a structure that is both energy-efficient and resilient to the coastal environment, satisfying the most stringent modern building standards while simplifying the construction process for the self-builder.
