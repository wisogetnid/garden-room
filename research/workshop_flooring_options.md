# Flooring Strategy: Heavy Workshop vs. Future Office (over UFH)

**The Challenge:** The 100mm concrete raft contains embedded Wet Underfloor Heating (UFH). The floor finish must have low thermal resistance (to let the heat through), withstand 200kg+ cast-iron point loads, survive dropped sharp tools (chisels), and ideally transition gracefully into a premium home office in the future.

## Option 1: Commercial Luxury Vinyl Tile (LVT) - *The Hybrid Winner*
LVT is a highly compressed composite vinyl that mimics the exact texture and grain of engineered oak or stone, but performs like industrial plastic. It must be **fully glued down** to the concrete using high-temperature UFH adhesive.
*   **Pros:** 
    *   *UFH Transfer:* Exceptional. At only 2.5mm thick, heat transfers almost instantly.
    *   *Durability:* Commercial-grade LVT (e.g., Karndean or Amtico with a 0.55mm+ wear layer) withstands immense static point loads without denting.
    *   *Aesthetics:* Looks like a premium £100/sqm oak floor, making it 100% ready for the future "Home Office" phase.
*   **Cons:** It can still be gouged if a 200kg cast-iron machine with sharp feet is *dragged* roughly across it. Machines should be placed on rubber isolation pads (Sylomer) anyway.
*   **Cost:** ~£30 - £50 per m² + adhesive. (£750 - £1,100 total).

## Option 2: Polished & Densified Concrete - *The Hardcore Industrial*
Instead of adding a floor, you simply treat the power-floated structural slab with a Lithium Silicate densifier and a polyurethane sealer.
*   **Pros:** 
    *   *UFH Transfer:* Perfect (100% efficiency).
    *   *Durability:* Practically indestructible. You can drag machinery across it all day.
    *   *Cost:* The cheapest option by far. Sealer and densifier cost ~£150 for the whole room.
*   **Cons:** 
    *   *Ergonomics:* It is brutally hard on the knees and lower back when standing at a workbench for 8 hours. 
    *   *Tool Damage:* If you drop a £100 Lie-Nielsen chisel, the concrete will instantly snap the blade. 
    *   *Aesthetics:* May feel too cold/industrial when converted to an office.

## Option 3: Interlocking Heavy-Duty PVC Tiles (e.g., Ecotile / R-Tile)
7mm thick solid PVC tiles designed for commercial garages and forklift traffic. They tap together like puzzle pieces directly over the concrete.
*   **Pros:** 
    *   *Tool Safety:* The PVC absorbs impacts. Dropped tools bounce rather than break.
    *   *Repairability:* If you somehow destroy a tile, you pop it out and replace it for £7.
    *   *Flexibility:* When you convert the room to an office in 5 years, you can literally lift the entire floor up, sell it on eBay, and lay a luxury carpet or oak floor.
*   **Cons:** 
    *   *UFH Transfer:* PVC is an insulator. It will slightly slow down the response time of the UFH and require the ASHP to run a degree or two hotter to push the heat through.
    *   *Aesthetics:* Distinctly "workshop/garage" looking.
*   **Cost:** ~£35 per m² (£700 total).

## Option 4: 2-Part Epoxy Resin
A seamless poured resin floor.
*   **Pros:** Waterproof, chemical resistant, easy to sweep sawdust, excellent UFH heat transfer. Can look incredibly premium (like a modern art gallery).
*   **Cons:** Highly slippery when covered in fine wood dust unless an anti-slip aggregate is broadcast into it. Very difficult to apply as a DIYer; if the concrete holds any residual moisture, the epoxy will bubble and fail.
*   **Cost:** ~£40 - £60 per m² (DIY kit) or £100+ applied.

## Recommendation
If the primary focus right now is a **woodworking workshop**, lay **Interlocking PVC Tiles (Option 3)**. They protect your expensive hand tools, handle the machinery weight, and you can simply lift them out and sell them when you eventually convert to an office. 

If you want a **"One-and-Done" floor** that looks like a high-end office immediately but can survive workshop abuse, go with **Fully-Bonded Commercial LVT (Option 1)**.
