# **Technical Engineering Report: Integrated External Wall Insulation and Zero-Bridge Foundation Systems for Coastal Timber-Frame Workshops**

The construction of high-performance timber-frame workshops in maritime environments, specifically Cowes on the Isle of Wight, demands an integrated approach to the building envelope that transcends traditional insulation methodologies. The convergence of severe coastal weather patterns, the specific hygrothermal requirements of woodworking activities, and the stringent thermal benchmarks of the 2026 Future Homes Standard necessitates a "Zero-Bridge" design philosophy. Central to this strategy is the seamless technical transition between the JACKODUR Atlas Extruded Polystyrene (XPS) foundation system and specialized External Wall Insulation (EWI) assemblies. This report provides an exhaustive engineering analysis of the materials, mechanical fixings, and finish systems required to establish a continuous thermal barrier, ensuring structural longevity and internal environmental stability in a saline, high-moisture atmosphere.

## **Maritime Environmental Stress and Site Context**

Cowes, located on the northern coast of the Isle of Wight, presents a microclimate characterized by high wind-driven rain, significant saline concentrations in the air, and fluctuating relative humidity driven by the proximity to the Solent. For a timber-frame workshop, these external factors represent a primary threat to structural health. The prevailing winds can drive horizontal rain against the building façade, forcing moisture into junctions that are inadequately detailed.1 Furthermore, the salt-laden air accelerates the corrosion of standard metal components and can degrade the alkalinity of traditional cementitious renders, leading to premature cracking and water ingress.1  
A woodworking workshop adds an internal dimension to this challenge. Wood is a hygroscopic material that constantly seeks equilibrium with its surroundings, absorbing or releasing moisture depending on the ambient humidity.4 Internal activities such as cutting, gluing, and the storage of kiln-dried lumber require a stable internal climate—ideally between 40% and 60% relative humidity—to prevent warping, cupping, or split joints in the final timber products.6 The thermal envelope must, therefore, be capable of managing a high internal vapor drive while simultaneously defending against aggressive external maritime moisture.8

## **Mechanics of the JACKODUR Atlas Zero-Bridge Foundation**

The "Zero-Bridge" thermal envelope begins at the ground level. Traditional strip foundations create a significant material-related thermal bridge where the masonry or timber structure meets the concrete slab.10 The JACKODUR Atlas system replaces this with a raft foundation entirely encapsulated in high-density XPS.11

### **Material Science of Extruded Polystyrene in Sub-Grade Applications**

The selection of Extruded Polystyrene (XPS) for the foundation is critical due to its closed-cell architecture. Unlike Expanded Polystyrene (EPS), which is composed of beads fused together with microscopic voids, XPS is produced through a continuous extrusion process.10 This creates a uniform skin and a cell structure that is virtually impermeable to liquid water, with long-term moisture absorption rates of less than 1% even under high-pressure conditions or repeated freeze-thaw cycles.10

| Physical Property | XPS (JACKODUR Atlas) | EPS (Standard EWI Grade) | Standard |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Thermal Conductivity ($\\lambda\_D$) | $0.034 \- 0.038\\ W/mK$ | $0.030 \- 0.045\\ W/mK$ | EN 13164 / 13163 |
| Moisture Absorption (Immersion) | $\< 1.0\\%$ | up to $10.0\\%$ | EN 12087 |
| Compressive Stress (10% Def.) | $300 \- 700\\ kPa$ | $70 \- 150\\ kPa$ | EN 826 |
| Vapor Resistance ($\\mu$) | $80 \- 250$ | $20 \- 40$ | EN 12086 |

The stability of XPS ensures that the thermal conductivity remains constant over the building's lifespan. If moisture were permitted to ingress, as is common in lower-grade EPS, the thermal performance would degrade by as much as 50% as water (with a high $\\lambda$ of $0.58\\ W/mK$) replaced the air in the material's voids.10 The JACKODUR Atlas system is certified for these demanding conditions, holding both BBA (20/5812) and Passive House Institute certifications.11

### **Interlocking Component Architecture**

The 100mm JACKODUR Atlas system utilized for this workshop context relies on a sophisticated interlocking tongue-and-groove geometry. The system comprises four primary components that eliminate the need for adhesives or mechanical fixings below the slab:

1. **Surface Elements:** Large-format boards that form the continuous horizontal insulation layer beneath the concrete raft.10  
2. **Side Elements:** Vertical perimeter components that define the slab edge and provide the primary thermal break between the ground and the building superstructure.10  
3. **Corner Edge Elements:** Pre-machined components that ensure thermal continuity at the most vulnerable geometric points of the foundation.10  
4. **Formwork Elements:** Vertical boards of varying heights (determined by the structural engineer) that act as permanent shuttering for the concrete pour.10

This "complete solution" ensures that the concrete slab—which acts as essential internal thermal mass for the workshop—remains entirely within the thermal envelope.11 This thermal mass helps regulate the internal temperature by absorbing heat during periods of activity and releasing it during the night, reducing the reliance on high-energy heating or cooling systems in the volatile Cowes climate.11

## **Transition Engineering: From Foundation to Timber-Frame EWI**

The most technically demanding aspect of the Zero-Bridge envelope is the transition between the 100mm XPS perimeter and the vertical External Wall Insulation. This junction must maintain thermal continuity while managing the different vapor profiles and structural loads of the ground and the wall.14

### **Technical Detail 01-015: Timber-Work and Perimeter Interface**

According to the JACKODUR technical drawings for timber-frame connections (Detail 01-015), the slab-to-wall interface must be treated as a multifaceted moisture defense layer.14  
The 100mm XPS side element extends upward to meet the wall structure. The timber-frame studs, typically sheathed in 15mm OSB/3, are positioned so that the EWI layer aligns precisely with the outer face of the XPS formwork.15 To prevent moisture wicking from the ground into the timber structure, a Damp Proof Course (DPC) must be installed at the base of the wall. The EWI starter track is then mounted just above this DPC.17  
If the EWI insulation must extend below the DPC for aesthetic or thermal reasons, a transition in material is mandatory. The section of EWI below the DPC should be composed of water-resistant XPS rather than the standard wall insulation (such as Mineral Wool or Wood Fibre).17 This area must be finished with a waterproof basecoat, such as Aquabase, and an impermeable mosaic render to withstand the splash-back of rainwater and potential ground saturation.17

### **Structural Airtightness and Vapor Sealing**

The Zero-Bridge envelope is not merely a thermal barrier but an airtightness boundary. The Polyethylene (PE) film used within the JACKODUR foundation system to protect the XPS from the concrete pour must be lapped and permanently sealed to the timber-frame’s internal Vapor Control Layer (VCL).10 This prevents internal workshop humidity from bypassing the thermal layers through convection, which would otherwise result in interstitial condensation at the cold side of the OSB sheathing.8

## **External Wall Insulation Systems: Material Selection for Timber Frame**

For a timber-frame workshop, the selection of the insulation core material for the EWI system is governed by the need to protect the organic substrate (timber and OSB) from decay. Two primary philosophies exist: the "Vapor-Open" (Breathing) approach and the "Vapor-Closed" (Occlusion) approach.8

### **High-Performance Mineral Wool (Rockwool Dual Density)**

In coastal Cowes, a dual-density mineral wool system is often the preferred choice for professional installers due to its fire performance and breathability. Mineral wool (stone wool) is manufactured by spinning molten volcanic rock into fibrous strands, resulting in a material that is inherently non-combustible with a Euroclass A1 rating.24  
The "Dual-Density" construction provides a technical advantage: the outer surface has a higher density to resist the impact of wind-driven debris and provide a stable base for the render, while the softer inner layer conforms to the minor irregularities of the OSB sheathing.24 From a moisture management perspective, mineral wool has a vapor resistance similar to that of still air ($\\mu \\approx 1$), allowing internal moisture to safely migrate outward through the insulation layer and the breathable render.26 This "vapor-open" characteristic is a critical failsafe for timber-framed buildings where moisture trapped within the structure can lead to fungal rot in as little as three days if the moisture content exceeds 25%.4

### **Wood Fibre Systems (Steico Protect)**

Steico Wood Fibre insulation boards (Protect or Special Dry) represent a high-performance alternative that aligns with the sustainable goals of many timber-frame projects. These boards are manufactured from coniferous wood fibers, utilizing the wood’s natural lignin as a binder in the "wet process" or a small amount of biogenic binder in the "dry process".32

| Performance Factor | Steico Protect DRY (M) | Rockwool Frontrock (DD) | Kingspan Kooltherm K15 |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Thermal Conductivity ($\\lambda$) | $0.043\\ W/mK$ | $0.036\\ W/mK$ | $0.021\\ W/mK$ |
| Specific Heat Cap. ($c$) | $2,100\\ J/kgK$ | $1,030\\ J/kgK$ | $1,400\\ J/kgK$ |
| Fire Classification | Euroclass E | Euroclass A1 | Euroclass C-s1, d0 |
| Vapor Openness | High | Exceptional | Low |
| Embodied Carbon | Negative (Stores $CO\_2$) | Moderate | High |

The significant advantage of wood fibre in a workshop environment is its specific heat capacity ($2,100\\ J/kgK$), which is more than double that of mineral wool.32 This provides a high "thermal mass effect" in the walls, known as decrement delay. It prevents the workshop from overheating on hot summer days by delaying the transfer of external heat into the building by several hours.8 For a woodworking workshop where temperature stability is vital for glue curing and timber stability, this buffering capacity is a major benefit.34

### **Phenolic Foam (Kingspan Kooltherm K15)**

Where space is at a premium and a thinner 100mm total wall profile is required to match the foundation, Kingspan Kooltherm K15 Rainscreen Board offers the highest thermal efficiency with a $\\lambda$ value of $0.021\\ W/mK$.37 However, phenolic foam is a vapor-closed material. In a timber-frame context, this system usually necessitates a "drained and ventilated cavity" between the insulation and the render carrier board to ensure that any incidental moisture can escape.9 Current NHBC guidance (Chapter 6.2) often precludes the use of façade systems that do not utilize a drained cavity for timber frames, particularly in high-exposure maritime zones like Cowes.9

## **Advanced Mechanical Fixing: The EJOT STR H System**

To secure the chosen EWI system to the timber studs through the 15mm OSB/3 sheathing, the selection of the mechanical fixing is paramount for maintaining the Zero-Bridge thermal integrity. Standard screws create point thermal bridges that can lead to "ghosting" (dark spots on the render where condensation forms over cold fastener heads).40

### **Engineering the Thermal Break**

The EJOT STR H is a specialized universal screw-in anchor designed for timber substrates that incorporates a plastic washer to provide a definitive thermal break.40 By using the "STR Principle," the installer can countersink the screw head into the insulation board. This is followed by the insertion of an STR Cap (made of EPS or Mineral Wool), creating a homogeneous surface for the render system.41  
The thermal efficiency of this fixing is measurable. The point thermal transmission ($\\chi$) of an STR H fixing is reduced to $0.001\\ W/K$ when countersunk.40 In a workshop with thousands of fixings, this reduction is essential to achieving a truly high-performance envelope.

### **Fixing Length and Embedment Calculations**

For the 100mm EWI requirement bridging the 100mm JACKODUR foundation, the length of the STR H fixing must account for the insulation thickness, the OSB sheathing, and the necessary embedment into the timber studs.40

| Component Layer | Thickness (mm) | Calculation Logic |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| EWI Insulation | $100$ | Primary thermal layer |
| OSB Sheathing | $15$ | Structural substrate |
| Safety Tolerance | $5$ | Countersinking depth |
| Minimum Embedment | $35$ | Engagement into timber stud |
| **Total Required Length** | **$155\\ mm$** | (Standard size: $160\\ mm$) |

Using a $160mm$ EJOT STR H ensures that at least $40mm$ of the $6mm$ diameter screw is engaged with the load-bearing timber studs.40 On OSB plates with a thickness $\\ge 16mm$, each fixing provides a design load ($F\_{ax,90,Rd}$) of $0.25\\ kN$, meaning that a standard density of 7 fixings per square meter can easily support even the heavier dual-density mineral wool systems.30

## **Coastal Finish Systems: Silicone vs. Acrylic Render**

The final defensive layer of the EWI system in Cowes must be resistant to the specific chemical and biological pressures of the maritime environment. Modern thin-coat renders have largely replaced traditional sand-and-cement mixes due to their superior flexibility and crack resistance.48

### **Silicone Polymer Chemistry in Maritime Zones**

Silicone render is widely regarded by professional contractors as the "gold standard" for coastal properties.48 Its chemical composition incorporates silicone resins that provide high water repellency while remaining vapor-permeable.48 This creates a "lotus effect," where water droplets bead up and roll off the surface, taking dirt, salt deposits, and atmospheric pollutants with them.48  
In Cowes, the primary benefits of silicone render include:

1. **Salt Spray Resistance:** The hydrophobic surface prevents saline water from being absorbed into the render matrix, where it could otherwise crystallize and cause expansion damage (salt bursting).1  
2. **UV Stability:** Coastal areas often experience higher UV intensities due to reflection off the water. Silicone renders are through-coloured with mineral pigments that are UV-stable, preventing the fading and chalking common in acrylic-based paints.48  
3. **Algae and Fungal Mitigation:** The damp, salt-laden atmosphere of the Solent is conducive to algae growth. Silicone render’s self-cleaning properties, combined with encapsulated biocides, drastically reduce the risk of green or red algae staining.1

### **Acrylic Render and Structural Movement**

Acrylic renders, which use acrylic plastic resins as a binding agent, offer the highest level of flexibility of all render types.48 They are exceptionally resistant to mechanical damage and the micro-movements inherent in timber-frame structures.49 However, acrylic render is significantly less breathable than silicone.50 In a woodworking workshop where internal vapor levels may be high, the use of a non-breathable acrylic render could trap moisture within the insulation layer, leading to the saturation of the OSB and the eventual failure of the structure.8 Therefore, for this specific context, a high-quality silicone render is technically superior.

## **Base Tracks and Wicking Prevention: uPVC vs. 316 Stainless Steel**

The starter track (or base track) at the bottom of the EWI system is a critical point of failure in coastal environments. It must support the weight of the insulation while preventing moisture from "wicking" upward from the foundation into the wall assembly.17

### **uPVC Starter Tracks**

uPVC (unplasticized polyvinyl chloride) starter tracks are frequently recommended for coastal projects because they are entirely immune to salt air corrosion.17 Furthermore, uPVC has very low thermal conductivity, ensuring that the starter track does not become a linear thermal bridge that bypasses the 100mm JACKODUR foundation insulation.56 Modern uPVC tracks are designed with an integrated mesh and a "drip edge" (or drip nose) that forces rainwater to fall away from the building rather than tracking back toward the substrate.17

### **Metallic Tracks in Maritime Contexts**

Aluminum starter tracks are common but are prone to oxidation in saline air unless they are powder-coated to a high standard.3 In aggressive coastal locations, 316 Marine Grade Stainless Steel is the only metallic option that can guarantee long-term durability.58 However, stainless steel tracks are significantly more expensive and, like aluminum, have a much higher thermal conductivity than uPVC.58  
For a 100mm EWI system on a timber-frame workshop, a high-impact uPVC starter track with a weeped drip edge and built-in weep holes is the optimal solution.60 This configuration allows any moisture that manages to penetrate behind the render to drain out effectively, preventing the "saturation zone" that often forms at the base of poorly detailed EWI systems.60

## **Internal Moisture Dynamics and Timber Health**

Managing the internal environment of a woodworking workshop is as much about protecting the building fabric as it is about protecting the wood projects being crafted. The Zero-Bridge envelope creates a "warm wall" construction where the structural timber and OSB are maintained at a temperature above the dew point.64

### **Equilibrium Moisture Content (EMC) Control**

The health of the timber frame is defined by its moisture content. Construction-grade timber and OSB should ideally be maintained below 16% moisture content to prevent the development of mold, and strictly below 20% to prevent the establishment of wood-decaying fungi.4

| Relative Humidity (RH) | Temperature (∘C) | Equilibrium Moisture Content (EMC) | Condition |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| $30\\%$ | $20$ | $6\\%$ | Dry / Brittle Risk |
| $50\\%$ | $20$ | $9 \- 10\\%$ | Ideal for Woodworking |
| $65\\%$ | $20$ | $12\\%$ | Safe for Structure |
| $85\\%$ | $20$ | $18 \- 20\\%$ | Fungal Risk Threshold |

The high-performance EWI system prevents the internal relative humidity from spiking in the winter. In poorly insulated workshops, the cold internal surfaces of the walls cause local air temperatures to drop, which in turn causes the relative humidity in those areas to rise toward 100% (condensation).8 By keeping the OSB warm with 100mm of external insulation, the risk of this localized high humidity is eliminated, maintaining the timber frame in a state of permanent dryness.65

### **Dust, Health, and Heating Systems**

A well-insulated workshop reduces the need for aggressive forced-air heating, which can circulate wood dust and lower the relative humidity to dangerous levels (below 30%).6 High-efficiency workshops often utilize the thermal mass of the slab—protected by the JACKODUR Atlas system—in conjunction with radiant heating.11 This provides a stable, dust-neutral heating method that supports a healthier environment for the woodworker and better results for finishing and gluing processes.7

## **Regulatory Compliance and Structural Warranty**

Achieving a Zero-Bridge envelope is not merely an engineering goal but a regulatory requirement under the evolving Future Homes Standard. By 2026, building regulations will target wall U-values of approximately $0.18\\ W/m^2K$ for new structures.9 For a timber-frame workshop, this typically requires a combination of high-performance insulation between the studs and a continuous external layer of at least 100mm.8

### **NHBC Standards and the Drained Cavity**

A critical conflict in timber-frame EWI design is the requirement for a "drained and ventilated cavity" under NHBC Standards Chapter 6.2.9 Traditional EWI systems where insulation is fixed directly to the OSB and rendered (the "direct-fix" method) are common on masonry buildings but are viewed as high-risk for timber structures in exposed maritime locations.9  
To satisfy both the Zero-Bridge thermal requirement and the warranty requirements for timber frames:

1. **Direct-Fix with Risk Mitigation:** Some systems use high-performance breathable membranes and specialized wood fibre boards that are hydrophobic but vapor-open, allowing the assembly to dry inward and outward without a cavity.35  
2. **Carrier Board Systems:** Modern professional systems now often utilize an A1-rated fiber-cement carrier board fixed over timber battens.9 The EWI insulation is placed between the studs and behind the carrier board, or in a continuous layer behind a ventilated façade.9

Given the Cowes context, the use of a dual-density mineral wool system fixed directly to the OSB with a high-performance silicone render is viable *if* the internal VCL is meticulously sealed and an "intelligent" moisture management membrane is used on the warm side of the structure.9

### **Fire Safety (Approved Document B)**

Fire safety in timber construction is under intense scrutiny. A mineral wool EWI system provides an A1 non-combustible shield that can withstand temperatures exceeding $1,000^\\circ C$, significantly increasing the building’s fire resistance and protecting the load-bearing timber studs from heat exposure.25 This is especially important for workshops where flammable dust and solvents may be present.7

## **Engineering Synthesis and Technical Recommendations**

To achieve a Zero-Bridge thermal envelope for a timber-frame workshop in Cowes that successfully bridges the 100mm JACKODUR Atlas foundation, the following engineering specifications are recommended:

### **Foundation and Sub-Structure**

The JACKODUR Atlas XPS system should be installed with 100mm surface and side elements to establish the baseline thermal continuity. All interlocking joints must be tight-fitted, and the internal PE film must be professionally lapped and sealed to the wall’s VCL to ensure an unbroken air barrier. The foundation slab must be poured in accordance with technical detail 01-015 to ensure the timber studs are thermally decoupled from the ground.10

### **EWI Wall Assembly**

A 100mm Dual-Density Mineral Wool system (such as Rockwool Frontrock) is recommended for its non-combustibility and exceptional vapor openness.24 This material effectively shifts the dew point away from the structural OSB and provides the high-performance thermal envelope required to match the foundation.65

### **Mechanical Fixings**

Fasten the insulation to the timber studs through the OSB/3 sheathing using $160mm$ EJOT STR H anchors.40 Utilize the countersunk "STR Principle" with mineral wool caps to eliminate point thermal bridges and prevent render "ghosting".41 Ensure a minimum stud embedment of $35mm$ to maintain structural integrity under high coastal wind loads.40

### **Coastal Render Finish**

The system must be finished with a premium silicone thin-coat render (e.g., SAS ProRend Colourtex or EWI Pro Silicone) for its self-cleaning properties, UV stability, and resistance to saline atmosphere.1 The render should be applied over a glass-fiber reinforced basecoat to accommodate the micro-movements of the timber frame.48

### **Base Detailing**

Install a high-impact uPVC starter track with a weeped drip nose just above the DPC level.17 This ensures that rainwater is shed away from the slab-to-wall interface and that the Zero-Bridge continuity is maintained without the thermal leakage of metallic tracks.56  
By integrating these specialized components into a single, cohesive system, the Cowes workshop will achieve a state-of-the-art thermal envelope that exceeds current building regulations, protects the structural timber from the aggressive maritime climate, and provides a stable, productive environment for high-precision woodworking for the next 50 to 60 years.9
