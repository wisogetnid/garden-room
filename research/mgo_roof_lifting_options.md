# Strategy: Lifting 150kg MgO SIP Roof Panels on a Restricted Site

**The Challenge:** A 175mm thick Magnesium Oxide (MgO) SIP roof panel spanning 4 meters weighs approximately **120kg to 150kg**. A 30m narrow garden path makes crane access impossible. Lifting these panels 2.5 meters into the air safely requires mechanical advantage or strategic panel sizing. 

## Option A: Narrow-Format "Solo" SIPs (The Design Solution)
Instead of ordering standard 1200mm wide roof panels, specify **600mm wide** panels from the manufacturer (e.g., MTL Ltd).
*   **How it works:** The manufacturer rips the SIPs in half and provides twice as many insulated splines. 
*   **Weight reduction:** The panels drop to **~60kg - 75kg** each. 
*   **Pros:** Two strong people can easily slide these up a set of scaffold boards leaning against the wall plate. No heavy machinery required. Maximum safety.
*   **Cons:** You have twice as many joints to glue, screw, and tape on the roof, slightly increasing the risk of an airtightness leak if not meticulous. Slightly higher material cost for the extra splines.
*   **Cost:** £0 in hire fees. 

## Option B: The Material Lift (The "Genie Lift")
A Material Lift (often called a SLC or Genie Lift) is a manually operated, hand-winched forklift designed specifically for restricted access.
*   **How it works:** The lift rolls through a standard doorway. You manually place the 150kg panel onto the forks at ground level. You hand-crank the winch to raise the forks 2.5m into the air until the panel is hovering over the wall plate. You then slide it horizontally into position.
*   **Pros:** Eliminates 90% of the physical lifting danger. Allows precise positioning before dropping the panel onto the glued spline. Can lift up to 300kg.
*   **Cons:** Needs a relatively flat, hard surface to roll on (plywood sheets over grass/mud works). The forks might not be long enough to balance a 4m panel perfectly; requires two people to stabilize it while winching.
*   **Cost:** ~£80 - £120 for a weekend hire. 

## Option C: Scaffold Ramps & Winch (The "Pyramid" Method)
*   **How it works:** You erect a heavy-duty scaffold tower inside or outside the building. Lean two strong scaffold boards from the ground to the 2.5m wall plate. Place the panel at the bottom. Attach a 12V or manual block-and-tackle winch to the top scaffold rail, strap the panel, and winch it up the ramp. 
*   **Pros:** Cheap if you already have the scaffolding for the build. Very controlled ascent.
*   **Cons:** Highly labor-intensive to set up the rigging safely. Once at the top, you still have to awkwardly pivot a 150kg panel from a 45-degree angle to flat, which is the most dangerous moment where fingers get crushed.
*   **Cost:** ~£50 for winch hire + Scaffold tower hire (~£80/week).

## Option D: Lightweight Cassette Roof (The Structural Pivot)
Abandoning the MgO SIPs for the roof entirely and switching to an I-Joist or C24 Timber "Warm Roof" built stick-by-stick.
*   **How it works:** You carry individual 15kg timber joists up a ladder, fix them in place, then lay 18mm OSB, then rigid PIR insulation on top.
*   **Pros:** Completely solo-build friendly. No single component weighs more than 25kg. 
*   **Cons:** Much slower (adds 2-3 days). You lose the monolithic airtightness of a SIP envelope. You have to manually cut and tape the vapour control layer across the ceiling.
*   **Cost:** Timber/PIR materials are generally cheaper than MgO SIPs, offsetting the labor time.

## Recommendation for the Master Plan
**Option A (Narrow-Format 600mm SIPs) combined with Option B (Material Lift).** 
By ordering 600mm panels, the weight drops to a manageable 70kg. Hiring a £100 Material Lift ensures nobody strains their back or drops a panel during the critical gluing phase.
