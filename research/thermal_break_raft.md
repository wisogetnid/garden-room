# Structural Thermal Breaks in Floating Insulated Raft Foundations

## Overview
A "floating insulated raft" (where the concrete slab is cast inside an EPS/XPS "tub" or L-shaped perimeter elements) provides continuous insulation beneath and around the foundation, separating the entire concrete slab from the ground. However, even with the concrete entirely within the thermal envelope, structural thermal breaks (such as Compacfoam, Marmox Thermoblock, or Foamglas) are frequently utilized at the ground-to-wall interface (underneath the timber sole plate or masonry starter course).

## Technical Reasons for Use

### 1. Mitigation of Flanking Transmission (Cold Bridging via the Sole Plate)
* **Confidence Rating:** 5
* **Explanation:** In a timber frame or SIP structure, the wall's base track/sole plate sits directly on the concrete slab. While the concrete is insulated from the *ground* by the EPS tub, heat from the interior room easily enters the concrete slab. If the timber sole plate sits directly on this concrete near the perimeter, the heat travels through the slab and *up* into the timber plate, partially bypassing the thick wall insulation. Timber has a thermal conductivity ($\lambda$) of ~0.13-0.16 W/mK, making it a mild thermal bridge compared to modern insulation ($\lambda$ ~0.02-0.04 W/mK). Substituting the timber base track with a structural thermal break ($\lambda$ ~0.04 W/mK) drastically cuts this flanking transmission route.

### 2. Capillary Break and Damp Proofing (DPC)
* **Confidence Rating:** 5
* **Explanation:** Concrete slabs retain construction moisture for months and can absorb minor spills or condensation from inside the thermal envelope. Standard DPC membranes are thin and easily punctured. Products like Foamglas (cellular glass) and Marmox Thermoblock (XPS core with polymer concrete facings) are completely impermeable to water and possess zero capillarity. They provide an incredibly robust physical moisture barrier, keeping the vulnerable timber sole plate bone-dry while providing thermal resistance.

### 3. Geometric Alignment and Structural Support over Soft EPS
* **Confidence Rating:** 4
* **Explanation:** To achieve Passivhaus standards (linear thermal transmittance / $\Psi$-value $\le$ 0.01 W/mK), the wall insulation must perfectly align with the foundation perimeter insulation (the EPS L-element upstand). Often, the external wall (or its external insulation layer) needs to sit partially over the soft EPS perimeter upstand rather than the structural concrete. Standard EPS cannot take the point loads of a structural wall. High-density structural thermal breaks (like Compacfoam, which can withstand high compressive loads) can span the gap or cantilever over the EPS, providing rigid structural support exactly where the wall needs to sit to maintain continuous insulation geometry.

### 4. Thermal Bypass at the Slab Edge Junction
* **Confidence Rating:** 5
* **Explanation:** Even if the concrete is insulated on the side by an EPS upstand, the 90-degree corner where the slab, EPS upstand, and wall meet is mathematically the weakest point of the envelope due to multi-dimensional heat flow. Placing a highly insulative structural block precisely at this juncture interrupts the heat path at the exact bottleneck, ensuring the internal surface temperature of the floor-wall junction remains high enough to prevent condensation and mold growth, a critical requirement for Passivhaus certification.

## Common Materials Compared
* **Marmox Thermoblock:** Extruded polystyrene (XPS) core with load-bearing epoxy-concrete cylinders. Excellent for masonry or timber. Extremely high compressive strength. Highly impermeable. (Rating: 5)
* **Compacfoam:** High-density expanded polystyrene (EPS). Behaves like wood (can be screwed/sawn). Excellent for timber frame/SIP sole plates and cantilevered door thresholds. High thermal resistance ($\lambda$ ~0.04 W/mK). (Rating: 5)
* **Foamglas (Cellular Glass):** 100% glass foam. Completely impervious to moisture and vapor. High compressive strength but brittle. Ideal as a permanent, thermally broken damp-proof course. (Rating: 4)

## Conclusion
Even in a fully insulated raft, the concrete acts as a massive thermal reservoir connected to the room air. A structural thermal break is required to decouple the structural wall (timber or masonry) from the concrete slab, preventing heat from flanking *around* the wall insulation, ensuring continuous insulation geometry, and providing an exceptionally durable damp-proof course.
