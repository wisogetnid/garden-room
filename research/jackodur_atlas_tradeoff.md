# Jackodur Atlas Insulation Thickness: Passivhaus vs. Self-Builder Reality

## Passivhaus Standard Requirements
*   **Target U-Value:** Passivhaus typically requires a floor U-value of $\le 0.15 \text{ W/(m}^2\text{K)}$, ideally nearing $0.10 \text{ W/(m}^2\text{K)}$.
*   **Jackodur Atlas (XPS) Thermal Conductivity:** $\lambda \approx 0.035 \text{ W/(mK)}$.
*   **Required Thickness:** To achieve a U-value of $0.11 - 0.14 \text{ W/(m}^2\text{K)}$, you need **250mm to 300mm** of Jackodur Atlas floor insulation beneath the slab.

## The Self-Builder Problem (No Machinery)
For a 4x5m (20m²) garden room, the excavation depth for a full 300mm Passivhaus-compliant raft is substantial:
*   Sub-base (MOT Type 1): 150mm
*   Sand blinding: 25mm
*   Jackodur Insulation: 300mm
*   *Note: Concrete slab (100mm) usually sits partially above ground to create the 150mm splash zone, so it doesn't add to excavation depth.*
*   **Total Excavation Depth:** ~475mm.
*   **Volume of Earth to Move:** $20\text{m}^2 \times 0.475\text{m} = 9.5\text{ m}^3$ of solid earth.
*   **Bulking Factor & Weight:** When dug, soil expands by ~30% (yielding ~12.5m³ of loose spoil). At ~1.6 tonnes per m³, a self-builder would be hand-digging, wheelbarrowing, and skipping **over 20 tonnes of heavy soil**. This is brutal, back-breaking work without a mini-excavator.

## The Trade-off Recommendation: 100mm to 150mm Thickness
For a self-builder excavating by hand, the **sweet spot is 100mm or 150mm Jackodur Atlas**. 

### 1. Excavation Reduction (The 100mm setup)
*   Sub-base: 100mm
*   Sand blinding: 25mm
*   Jackodur Insulation: 100mm
*   **Total Excavation Depth:** ~225mm.
*   **Volume to Move:** $20\text{m}^2 \times 0.225\text{m} = 4.5\text{ m}^3$ solid earth ($\approx 6\text{ m}^3$ loose, or **~9-10 tonnes**). This cuts the manual labor by more than half, making it a realistic weekend job for 1-2 people.

### 2. Thermal Performance Trade-off
*   **100mm Jackodur:** Yields a U-value of approximately **0.32 - 0.35 W/(m²K)**.
*   **150mm Jackodur:** Yields a U-value of approximately **0.22 - 0.24 W/(m²K)**.
*   *Context:* While this misses the strict Passivhaus standard (0.15), $0.22 \text{ W/(m}^2\text{K)}$ is incredibly efficient for a garden room. Garden rooms have small volumes and are usually heated intermittently (evenings/weekends). The heat loss through a 100-150mm XPS floor is so minimal that the extra energy required to heat the room will cost only pennies per month, whereas hiring machinery or paying for labor/skips to remove 20 tonnes of soil costs hundreds of pounds upfront.

## Conclusion
**Confidence Rating: 5**
For a self-builder digging by hand, drop the strict Passivhaus 300mm requirement. **Specify 100mm or 150mm Jackodur Atlas.** It reduces excavation weight by 10+ tonnes, slashes skip hire costs, saves your back, and still provides exceptional thermal performance for an intermittently heated space.
